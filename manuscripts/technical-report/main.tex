\title{How far does a learned surrogate of a flowsheet simulator
transfer across ores? A closed-circuit, size-by-mineral engine, with
cases and ore groups held out}
\author{Felipe Santibáñez-Leal (ORCID: 0000-0002-0150-3246)}
\date{}
\begin{document}
\maketitle
\headerblock
\begin{abstract}


Learned surrogates of process simulators are often evaluated on random states from simulated plants they have already seen. We compare interpolation, leave-one-case-out and leave-one-ore-group-out evaluation for five models trained on a steady-state mineral-processing simulator. The simulator tracks mineral mass over 63 size classes, water and closed grinding circuits, with unit-level balance audits and explicit misses against published examples. On a 3,072-state design over twelve authored cases, the multilayer perceptron has an interpolation recovery RMSE of 3.38 percentage points. Its magnetite transfer forecast is wholly outside a declared gross-range diagnostic and remains a rejected fold with null error metrics. Over the eleven case folds admissible for all models, mean recovery RMSE is 6.86 points for the perceptron, 8.67 for gradient boosting and 9.40 for the Gaussian process. These conditional results do not describe performance on the excluded case. Four of five neural seeds reject magnetite; the remaining seed retains a 217.18-point error, exposing instability that an interpolation score conceals. The perceptron's recovery R² is 0.795 on five chalcopyrite cases held out together and -10.2 on two gold-in-pyrite cases. Group-held-out log-upgrade median R² is negative for every model. Results therefore depend on the target, held-out unit, admissibility diagnostic and cohort denominator. The cases are authored; these results do not establish performance on operating plants.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

A surrogate of a flowsheet simulator is attractive for the same reason
as any surrogate: the simulator is slow and the surrogate is fast, so it
can sit inside an optimizer, a sweep or an interactive tool. Whether it
can stand in for the simulator depends on the question it is asked.
Inside the envelope it was trained on, an interpolation score answers
that question. A digital twin is asked something harder: what happens
with a different ore, a harder or finer feed, another plant. That is
transfer, and an interpolation score says nothing about it.

This work measures both on the same models, with a simulator whose
answers are known exactly and can be reproduced. Its contributions are
three:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  an open flowsheet engine that represents streams by size and mineral,
  solves closed circuits, audits every balance from its output streams,
  is checked against published examples with every miss stated, and is
  reproduced by a line-by-line second implementation;
\item
  a test protocol that scores learned surrogates of it by interpolation,
  by leaving one case out and by leaving one ore group out over twelve
  authored plants in four circuit families, with interval coverage and
  an out-of-envelope guard;
\item
  a measured transfer failure and seed instability on magnetite,
  conditional comparisons that retain the failed fold, distinct results
  for whole-case and ore-group holdouts, and a reconstruction guard
  whose missed feature shifts remain visible by input and distance.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Process model}\label{process-model}

The record reproduces all 96 committed states within 1e-6 in the browser
port and all 96 committed states close within 1e-9 at every unit in the
Python engine. The port shares constants and is a translation check, not
an independent validation of physical assumptions.

\subsection{Representation}\label{representation}

Every stream holds, for each mineral, a vector of mass flows (t/h) over
one grid of 63 size classes on a fourth-root-of-two progression from 150
mm to a pan below 3.24 µm, and a water flow. Assays are computed from
mineral masses and the minerals' element contents (from their formulas
and the IUPAC atomic weights); no grade is stored, so a grade changes
only when a mass does. Liberation is described per valuable mineral by
size (King 1979): a liberated fraction, and composites whose content and
host are declared.

\subsection{Comminution}\label{comminution}

The crusher is the Whiten form (Duarte et al.~2021), with a strictly
lower-triangular breakage matrix \(\mathbf{B}\) and a classification
function \(\mathbf{C}\) set by the closed-side setting:

\[
\mathbf{p}=(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{C})(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{B}\mathbf{C})^{-1}\mathbf{f}
\]

The ball mill is an energy-specific population balance (Herbst and
Fuerstenau 1980) in the form of the Moly-Cop tools: three perfect mixers
of volume fractions 0.70, 0.15 and 0.15 share the breakage operator
\(\mathbf{D}=(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{B})\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{S}^{E})\),
so the mill's inverse transfer at an energy per pass \(e\) is a cubic
polynomial in \(\mathbf{D}\), and the closed circuit with the cyclone
returning a fraction \(C_i\) of each class solves

\[
\begin{aligned}
\left[\mathbf{T}^{-1}(e)-\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{C})\right]\mathbf{p}
  &=\mathbf{f},\\
\mathbf{T}^{-1}(e)
  &=\mathbf{I}+c_1e\mathbf{D}+c_2e^2\mathbf{D}^2+c_3e^3\mathbf{D}^3
\end{aligned}
\]

per mineral. Two scalar unknowns, the energy per pass that meets the
target P80 and the host cut that holds the design circulating load, are
found by the Illinois method. The energy per pass is per tonne of mill
feed, so the mill draws \(e\,m\), with \(m\) the fresh feed plus the
cyclone underflow (less what a gravity bleed takes from it); when that
exceeds the installed power, the circuit runs at installed power,
\(e\,m(e)=P_{\mathrm{inst}}\) solved as a fixed point, with a coarser
product, and says so. The reported grinding energy is that net energy
per tonne of fresh feed, \(\frac{e\,m}{F}\); the crushing energy is
Bond's with the crushing work index, and the Bond requirement
\(E=10W_i\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{P}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{F}}\right)\) gives the
operating work index and the efficiency ratio beside it, with Rittinger
and Kick calibrated to agree with Bond at a reference reduction and
never added to it (Bond 1952; GMG 2021).

\subsection{Classification and
separation}\label{classification-and-separation}

Hydrocyclones follow Plitt (1976): a Rosin-Rammler partition

\[
y(d)=R_f+(1-R_f)\left[1-\exp\!\left(-\ln 2\left(\frac{d}{d_{50c}}\right)^m\right)\right]
\]

with a water bypass \(R_f\) from the water balance and a cut corrected
for each mineral's density, so dense minerals classify as coarser
particles and circulate. Plitt's sizing of the cyclone cluster is
uncalibrated and fails its published check (section 3), so the cluster's
count and pressure are reported as an estimate, never as a result.
Flotation banks are perfect mixers in series; the rate constant of each
particle class follows from the bubble surface area flux
\(S_b=\frac{6J_g}{D_{32}}\) (Gorain et al.~1997, 1999) and a size window
(Trahar 1981), a composite floats at the liberated rate scaled by its
exposed surface, \(P_{\mathrm{comp}}=P_V\phi^{\frac{2}{3}}\) with
\(\phi\) the valuable mineral's volume share, entrainment follows
Savassi et al.~(1998), and cleaner and recleaner tails return to the
preceding bank. Each cell's residence is its pulp volume, less the gas
hold-up, over the flow that leaves it as tail, which depends on what the
cell floats, so it is solved per cell as a fixed point:

\[
\begin{aligned}
r_j&=\frac{k\tau_j+\mathrm{ENT}\,w_j}{1+k\tau_j+\mathrm{ENT}\,w_j},\\
\tau_j&=\frac{V(1-\varepsilon_g)}{Q_{\mathrm{tail},j}},\\
R_N&=1-\prod_j(1-r_j)
\end{aligned}
\]

Gold follows the gravity-recoverable gold (GRG) model of Laplante,
Woodcock and Noaparast as Vincent (1997) states it: the gold is a GRG
component with its own size distribution, from a GRG test, plus a
non-GRG component; the GRG's cyclone cut takes a density correction with
an exponent of 1.0, fitted to measured GRG partitions where Stokes would
give 0.5; the gravity unit on a bleed of the cyclone underflow, or of
the mill discharge, recovers GRG by size at a per-pass recovery
\(R(d)=R_{\max}\left[1-\exp\!\left(-\left(\frac{d}{x_g}\right)^2\right)\right]\);
and grinding moves GRG to non-GRG at a slowed breakage rate (Banisi et
al.). Low-intensity magnetic drums capture liberated magnetite and
composites by their magnetite content; desliming cyclones discard slimes
ahead of flotation, with the water split following the solids sent down
at a declared underflow density.

\subsection{Balance closure and the
contract}\label{balance-closure-and-the-contract}

An independent recomputation calculates, from the output streams alone,
the closure of every mineral, every species and the water at every unit
and for the circuit, and of every size class at the units that do not
break particles. The operating point is thirteen inputs declared once,
with units, per-case bounds, steps, the families they apply to, a
cross-field rule and bilingual messages; the exported declaration is
interpreted identically by the engine's validator, the web service and
the browser, so a state is accepted or rejected with the same code
everywhere. A state the engine cannot solve, such as a classifier cut at
which the mill cannot draw its installed power, is refused with a reason
rather than answered.

\section{Verification}\label{verification}

\textbf{Balances.} All 96 committed states close within 1e-9 relative at
every unit and for the circuit, as recomputed from the stored streams by
the artifact checks.

\textbf{The second implementation.} A line-by-line TypeScript port runs
the engine in the browser. Re-simulating every committed state from its
own definition, it matches every metric, stream record, curve and
kinetic record within 1e-6 relative, with the same flags. It is a
translation by the same author that reads the same constant,
atomic-weight and mineral files, so it catches translation errors and
not modelling errors. An independent verification of the 0.08.001
implementation against its equations, one reviewer per part of the
engine and every finding verified, found defects in both engines, among
them the composite rate, the cell residence, the mill power basis and
the desliming water split; each was fixed, the engine's in both
languages, with a test that fails on the earlier code, and every number
below is from the fixed engine.

\section{Evaluation design}\label{evaluation-design}

\subsection{Authored circuits}\label{authored-circuits}

Twelve authored cases in four circuit families: nine copper, nickel,
zinc and refractory-gold rougher-cleaner flotation circuits
(\texttt{rougher}), free-milling gold with a gravity bleed
(\texttt{gravity\_rougher}), fine magnetite with magnetic drums instead
of flotation (\texttt{magnetic}), and phosphate with desliming ahead of
flotation (\texttt{deslime\_rougher}). Every parameter carries its unit
and either a source or the label authored; none is fitted to a plant.
Five of the twelve are copper sulphide plants on chalcopyrite, and the
two gold plants carry most of their gold in pyrite (55\%, beside
electrum in the free-milling ore and arsenopyrite in the refractory
one); the other five each have a payable and dominant carrier no other
case shares.

\subsection{Design, features and
targets}\label{design-features-and-targets}

For each case a scrambled Sobol sequence draws 256 states the contract
accepts over the case's whole envelope and two ore factors (liberation
size and floatability within their uncertainty half-widths), except that
magnetite varies liberation alone. There are 3072 states in all; the
engine simulates every state. Each state is described by 22 physical
features computed before simulation: the payable fraction, throughput
per installed megawatt, the grind, circulating load, water, crusher
setting and work index, the payable carriers' share-weighted liberation
size, composite content, density, floatability and dose ratio, the
rougher gas velocity, cells and volume per t/h, the gravity bleed, the
desliming cut and four circuit descriptors. The case identity is never a
feature. The targets are recovery (\%), the log10 upgrade ratio
(concentrate over head grade) and the total specific energy (kWh/t).

\subsection{Models and protocols}\label{models-and-protocols}

Ridge (alpha 1); a random forest (300 trees; Breiman 2001); histogram
gradient boosting (300 iterations; Friedman 2001); a Gaussian process
with a squared-exponential kernel with one length scale per feature and
a noise term, on a seeded subsample of 500 training rows (Rasmussen and
Williams 2006); and a perceptron of two hidden layers of 64 units,
trained by full-batch AdamW with early stopping on a 15\% validation
split (Loshchilov and Hutter 2019). Features are standardized on the
training rows of each split. Perceptron targets are standardized on
those same rows; the Gaussian process normalizes its own training
responses, while ridge and the tree models fit targets in their declared
units. Because the Gaussian process sees fewer rows, ridge, random
forest and gradient boosting are also refitted on the same 500 rows for
a separate comparison. The perceptron's ordinary scores retain the full
training subset. Because one training seed is one draw, the perceptron
is retrained with four more seeds for interpolation and each of the
twelve case holdouts. The two multi-case ore-group fits use the base
seed; no five-seed group-transfer stability claim follows from this
study.

The standardization fits the training subset alone. Numerically constant
training columns retain unit scale, including columns whose small
positive variance comes from summation roundoff. An unseen value is
therefore not amplified by division through a spurious near-zero scale.
Neither held-out features nor held-out targets determine the fitted
transformation. The interpolation comparison also retains a case-mean
baseline fitted on each case's training rows, and an R² denominator
based on within-case variation, separating case recognition from
response prediction.

\emph{Interpolation} holds out 20\% of the states inside every case
(2460 training, 612 test rows). \emph{Leave one case out} holds out each
case in turn; the models that predict a case never saw any of its
states, but they may have seen plants on the same ore. \emph{Leave one
ore group out} holds out every plant that shares the held-out plant's
payable and dominant carrier mineral: the five chalcopyrite plants
together (1792 training, 1280 test rows), the two gold plants together
(2560 training, 512 test rows), and each of the other five alone, where
it is the same fold as leaving one case out; seven groups in all. An
autoencoder (16, 6 and 16 units) trained on the features gives a guard:
its threshold is the 99th percentile of the reconstruction errors of its
validation rows. Its false-alarm rate is measured on the 612 held-out
in-envelope states. Its headline false-accept rate uses 11,016 probes,
each a held-out state pushed half a training span above the maximum of
one continuous feature. Separate acceptance curves repeat those probes
at six distances from 0.02 to 1.0 spans, independently above the maximum
and below the minimum.

\subsection{Prediction diagnostics and admissible
comparisons}\label{prediction-diagnostics-and-admissible-comparisons}

Shape and finiteness are checked before an error is computed. A further
gross-range diagnostic compares the prediction range with the target
range over the complete authored design, expanded by one observed span
on each side. For reference limits \(a\) and \(b\), the diagnostic
interval is

\[[a-(b-a),\ b+(b-a)].\]

This is a post-inference diagnostic and includes held-out target values.
It is not a training-support test, an operational constraint, an
independent transfer validation or a rule for selecting fitted
parameters. No prediction is clipped or refitted using these limits.
Because the test concerns disjoint ranges, it can miss extreme
individual predictions when other predictions overlap the interval;
passing it does not establish physical validity at every state.

A finite transfer forecast wholly outside that interval is retained as a
rejected transfer, with null RMSE, MAE and R², unmodified
observed/predicted/reference ranges, affected-row count and model,
target, protocol, fold and seed identifiers. The same check applies to
every constituent case of a grouped fold, so pooled overlap cannot
conceal an entirely invalid case. Nonfinite, shape, fitting and
programming failures abort evaluation; interpolation range failures also
abort. Finite poor forecasts inside the diagnostic envelope retain their
errors and negative R². Unexpected constant forecasts are identified
separately from the intentionally constant baseline.

Conditional summaries report attempted, scored and rejected counts
beside their error statistics. A missing admissible cohort has null
metrics. Comparisons across models additionally use the intersection of
folds admissible for every model, with all excluded identifiers
retained. These matched summaries remain conditional descriptions of
that intersection; they do not erase a model's failed transfers or rank
performance over the complete planned design. Complete per-fold,
per-case and per-seed evidence is retained in the release record.

\subsection{Energy and upgrade
targets}\label{energy-and-upgrade-targets}

The one-case-out median R² for total specific energy ranges from 0.652
(ridge) to 0.952 (gradient boosting); under ore-group holdout it ranges
from 0.617 (ridge) to 0.907 (Gaussian process). Log-upgrade median R² is
negative for every model under group holdout, so the recovery transfer
result does not generalize to grade-upgrade prediction. The held-out
protocols and target definitions remain separate in the record.

\subsection{Interval coverage}\label{interval-coverage}

Nominal 95\% Gaussian-process recovery intervals cover 90.7\% of
interpolation rows and 82.9\% of pooled case-held-out rows. The worst
case-fold coverage is 35.5\%, and four case folds cover fewer than 80\%
of their rows. These intervals use the GP's own mean and predictive
standard deviation. Coverage is target- and split-specific; the
authored-plant split does not establish calibrated intervals on real
plants.

\subsection{Reconstruction guard}\label{reconstruction-guard}

On the held-out interpolation experiment, the autoencoder flags 1.47\%
of 612 in-envelope states and accepts 15.51\% of 11,016 upward-shift
probes at half a training span. Its per-feature and per-distance
acceptance rates remain in \texttt{learning.json}. This validation guard
differs from the final guard refitted for deployment. A low
reconstruction error is a screening signal, not a process constraint, a
guarantee of physical safety or a substitute for the operating contract.

\section{Kinetic, optimization and sensitivity
studies}\label{kinetic-optimization-and-sensitivity-studies}

The engine produces kinetic, optimization and sensitivity records for
each variant. Kinetic fits, sensitivity and unscreened optimization use
the mechanistic engine. Screened optimization additionally consumes the
exported neural surrogate, reconstruction guard and Gaussian-process
interval.

For the paired search comparison, the recorded evaluation counter counts
distinct trial states in each fixed-weight multistart search's cache; a
state shared across starts is counted once. Screened and unscreened runs
use separate caches, and refused trial outcomes remain recorded. These
counters are captured before the subsequent warm-started weight-path
study. They exclude uncached base/endpoint audits, model training and
screening inference, so they are a bounded search-accounting measure
rather than a tally of every simulator invocation or a wall-clock
benchmark.

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Kinetic lumping.} Five lumped models fitted to a virtual batch
  test of the rougher feed, projected to the bank under the same
  residence distribution and compared with the exact bank recovery by
  true flotation, over 88 flotation states: mean absolute projection
  errors of 1.27 points for the gamma model, 1.81 for Kelsall, 2.80 for
  Klimpel, 4.76 for the stretched exponential and 7.56 for first order
  (Polat and Chander 2000; Vinnett and Waters 2025). The first-order
  projection falls below the exact result at every nominal state: a
  batch curve that is a mixture of rates is not one exponential.
\item
  \textbf{Constrained optimization.} A generalized pattern search with a
  progressive barrier from six starts (Torczon 1997; Audet and Dennis
  2006, 2009) finds a point within the grade, power and water
  constraints for 94 of the 96 variants; the two exceptions are the
  magnetite circuit's harder ore and higher throughput, where the grind
  is the only decision and the mill is already at installed power. The
  learned lane screens the search step (Booker et al.~1999): candidates
  are ranked by the perceptron's prediction and reach the engine only
  where the guard accepts their state and the Gaussian process's 95\%
  half-width on recovery is within 5 points, a novelty measure for the
  perceptron rather than an interval on what it ranks; the poll and the
  optimum stay engine results. The screen did not save engine work: over
  the 72 screened variants it used 26,367 engine evaluations versus
  22,487 without screening, 17.25\% more. It saved calls in none, used
  more in 68 variants and tied in four. Mean absolute recovery error
  averaged 0.66 percentage points across the 68 variants with evaluated
  proposals; four variants have no proposal-error estimate rather than
  zero error. Across 5,811 evaluated proposals its mean was 0.71 points,
  median 0.48 and maximum 38.16. Both procedures had the same terminal
  outcome in 65 variants: 63 identical feasible decisions and two
  matching infeasible outcomes. Seven feasible pairs selected different
  decisions, with recovered-metal differences no greater than 0.002653
  t/h (0.017\%). Five favored the unscreened decision and two the
  screened decision.
\item
  \textbf{Sensitivity.} Sobol indices of four uncertain ore properties
  at each nominal state (Saltelli et al.~2010; Herman and Usher 2017):
  floatability drives recovery in ten cases, liberation size drives
  concentrate grade in seven and the head grade in the other five, the
  work index drives grinding energy and the head grade drives recovered
  metal in all twelve.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Locked-cycle tests.} 52 GeoMet locked-cycle copper recoveries

mean, ridge, a random forest and a Gaussian process, under five folds of

zones no model separates either. The source has no grind, reagent or
residence information, so it cannot calibrate the engine's controls.

\textbf{The engine on the same samples.} The 52 samples also run through
the engine as inputs, each on its own assays in the soft copper
porphyry's circuit at that case's operating point; nothing in the engine
is fitted to them. The work index comes from the nearest comminution
sample in the hole (42 samples) or the deposit median (10), and spans
15.2 to 26.1 kWh/t against the case's 11.0. Most of the copper is
bornite and chalcocite, allocated by a sulphur-limited normative
mineralogy, one choice in a family of allocations; the two minerals
float at declared ratios to chalcopyrite (0.8, and chalcocite at 1.5
times bornite, a choice the GeoMet data do not bound). Every sample runs
the mill at installed power, and the engine's recovery falls short of
the locked-cycle test by 22.6 points on average (RMSE 24.1 points; 2.9
to 41.1 points below). That gap is mostly the host circuit's size: the
samples are harder than the circuit's design ore, so at 720 t/h the
product is coarse. With the circuit sized for the case's 150 µm target
the gap is +0.2 points, and it runs from +10.0 to -20.8 points as the
assumed laboratory grind goes from 75 to 300 µm, which the open data do
not give. At each sample's own target-grind throughput it is +4.0
points, of which 3.8 come from the longer flotation residence at the
lower throughput rather than from the grind. At every assumed grind the
engine carries no information about which sample recovers more (Pearson
r between 0.03 and 0.06). It is a comparison, not a calibration: the
circuit, the breakage, the liberation and the flotation are authored for
another ore, and a locked-cycle test is not a plant.

\textbf{Plant hours.} One iron-ore plant's reverse flotation record
(Kaggle dataset 6294, CC0; 737,453 rows) gives hourly laboratory silica
beside 21 feed, reagent, pulp and column sensors. Dropping the 310 hours
whose silica label was interpolated leaves 3,701 pairs of consecutive
hours. Scored on three future windows after at least 24 hours of
embargo, the previous assay alone forecasts the next hour's silica with
a mean absolute error of 0.464 points, and the best sensor-only model,
ridge, is 0.001 points below the training mean's 0.766. In 461 of those
pairs the laboratory value is one carried over unchanged for three or
more consecutive hours; without them the previous assay's error is 0.510
points, still the lowest, and ridge is 0.014 points above the training
mean. The engine has no reverse cationic flotation family, so these
hours are shown and never simulated.

\section{Discussion and limitations}\label{discussion-and-limitations}

The test bounds transfer between authored plants. A thirteenth authored
plant is not a real one: the engine is a steady-state model with
authored parameters, no froth stability, no dynamics and no prices, and
its twelve plants share its structural assumptions. That is a feature of
the design, since the teacher's answer is exact and reproducible, and a
limit of the conclusion, since a real plant can depart from all twelve
at once.

Four interpretation limits emerge from this simulator study. First,
interpolation and transfer answer different questions: a good
interpolation score coexists with a rejected transfer and a large finite
error under another seed. Second, the held-out unit changes the
evidence. Sharing a dominant mineral does not make whole-case and
whole-group tests interchangeable; the two gold cases expose a large
grouped error. Third, admissibility diagnostics change the scored
denominator. A lower conditional mean cannot be presented as an
improvement over a complete-design mean, and even a matched comparison
remains conditional on the shared admissible folds. Fourth,
reconstruction errors miss some controlled feature shifts, so their
acceptance rates need the feature, distance and tested population beside
them. These observations motivate a measured study on real operating
periods; they do not prove the same rankings or rates on a plant.

A calibrated study would replace the authored targets by measured
metallurgy across operating periods, calibrate the engine on some
periods and hold out an entire period and an ore family, and score both
point error and whether the surrogate preserves the ranking of operating
decisions.

\section{Reproducibility}\label{reproducibility}

The repository is https://github.com/fsantibanezleal/CAOS\_OreFlow
(MIT). The engine, the bake, the records, the browser port, the service
and the documentation are versioned together; the source, records and
computational environment are versioned with the software release.
\texttt{./scripts/setup.ps1} builds the environments and
\texttt{./scripts/precompute.ps1} regenerates every record. On a
workstation with 32 logical cores and an RTX 4070 Laptop GPU, the
pipeline took 5643 s for the cases on 8 workers and 4813 s for the
learned lane. These are run-specific wall times. The final MLP fit used
CUDA for a simulator study over authored cases; this computation does
not establish plant-calibrated performance. Independent evaluation folds
and neural seed runs can execute in bounded spawned workers. External
checkpoints are accepted only under the same scientific source, JSON
constants, release version, case definitions, operating contract,
settings, dependency versions and compute-runtime fingerprint. Each
checkpoint has a verified JSON checksum and scientific diagnostics;
corrupt or foreign records fail rather than being silently reused.
Execution metadata distinguishes planned, computed and resumed jobs.
Final deployed-model fitting and ONNX parity checks remain part of the
completed bake. Checkpoint reuse within that identical candidate is
distinct from the development-only reuse of an earlier release's
learning record, which is rejected for publication.
\texttt{./scripts/smoke.ps1} runs the checks: the guards, the Python and
frontend suites and the build, with the counts of each release in
\texttt{docs/release-verification.md}. The workbench at
https://oreflow.ml.fasl-work.com runs the engine in the browser on any
state of any case.
