\title{How far does a learned surrogate of a flowsheet simulator
transfer across ores? A closed-circuit, size-by-mineral engine, with
cases and ore groups held out}
\author{Felipe Santibáñez-Leal (ORCID: 0000-0002-0150-3246)}
\date{}
\begin{document}
\maketitle
\headerblock
\begin{abstract}


Learned surrogates of process simulators are often evaluated on random states from simulated plants they have already seen. We compare interpolation, leave-one-case-out and leave-one-ore-group-out evaluation for five models trained on a steady-state mineral-processing simulator. The simulator tracks mineral mass over 63 size classes, water and closed grinding circuits, with unit-level balance audits and explicit misses against published examples. On a 3,072-state design over twelve authored cases, the multilayer perceptron has an interpolation recovery RMSE of 3.38 percentage points. Its magnetite transfer forecast is wholly outside a declared gross-range diagnostic and remains a rejected fold with null error metrics. Over the eleven case folds admissible for all models, mean recovery RMSE is 6.86 points for the perceptron, 8.67 for gradient boosting and 9.40 for the Gaussian process. These conditional results do not describe performance on the excluded case. Four of five neural seeds reject magnetite; the remaining seed retains a 217.18-point error, exposing instability that an interpolation score conceals. The perceptron's recovery R² is 0.795 on five chalcopyrite cases held out together and -10.2 on two gold-in-pyrite cases. Group-held-out log-upgrade median R² is negative for every model. Results therefore depend on the target, held-out unit, admissibility diagnostic and cohort denominator. The cases are authored; these results do not establish performance on operating plants.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

A surrogate of a flowsheet simulator is attractive for the same reason
as any surrogate: the simulator is slow and the surrogate is fast, so it
can sit inside an optimizer, a sweep or an interactive tool. Whether it
can stand in for the simulator depends on the question it is asked.
Inside the envelope it was trained on, an interpolation score answers
that question. A digital twin is asked something harder: what happens
with a different ore, a harder or finer feed, another plant. That is
transfer, and an interpolation score says nothing about it.

This work measures both on the same models, with a simulator whose
answers are known exactly and can be reproduced. Its contributions are
three:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  an open flowsheet engine that represents streams by size and mineral,
  solves closed circuits, audits every balance from its output streams,
  is checked against published examples with every miss stated, and is
  reproduced by a line-by-line second implementation;
\item
  a test protocol that scores learned surrogates of it by interpolation,
  by leaving one case out and by leaving one ore group out over twelve
  authored plants in four circuit families, with interval coverage and
  an out-of-envelope guard;
\item
  a measured transfer failure and seed instability on magnetite,
  conditional comparisons that retain the failed fold, distinct results
  for whole-case and ore-group holdouts, and a reconstruction guard
  whose missed feature shifts remain visible by input and distance.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{sloppypar}
Interpolation, whole-case holdout and ore-group holdout estimate different
transfer regimes; the magnetite fold remains rejected, and conditional means
use only the eleven admissible folds rather than combining unlike decisions.

Published comminution and simulator studies provide context for the
controlled engine comparison; their plant-specific results are not
transferred as OreFlow calibration data \cite{whiten1972,koh2021}.

Grinding and flotation couple particle-size transformation to
classification, water circulation and mineral-specific recovery. Finer
grinding can improve liberation while increasing energy and changing the
flotation feed. A control selected from one unit response may therefore
violate a downstream constraint.

OreFlow records these couplings by stream and unit. It offers a controlled
basis for method comparisons and tests how validation units affect
conclusions; it does not replace plant sampling or metallurgical accounting.

The report distinguishes numerical closure within implemented equations,
reproduction of external reference cases and predictive agreement on
held-out records. Passing one category does not imply passing another.
\end{sloppypar}

\section{Problem formulation}\label{problem-formulation}

Let \(c\) index one of twelve authored circuit cases and let \(v\) index
one of its declared operating variants. OreFlow evaluates the map

\[z=\mathcal{E}(c,u;\theta),\]

where \(u\) is the case's operating point and \(\theta\) contains its
mineralogy, size distribution, liberation, density, hardness and
equipment declarations. The output \(z\) contains size-by-mineral
streams, water flows, equipment loads and derived assays. A call returns
a converged steady state or a structured refusal. Refusal is a
first-class outcome: it is not a zero recovery and cannot be silently
omitted from a denominator.

For mineral \(m\), unit feed rate \(F_m\) and output rates \(O_{m,k}\),
conservation requires

\[r_m=F_m-\sum_k O_{m,k}=0.\]

Water has a separate balance. Recovery is the payable mineral in the
selected product divided by payable feed; grade is elemental payable
mass divided by total concentrate solids. Because the two denominators
differ, they are not interchangeable. Increasing mass pull can raise
recovery while lowering grade. The engine checks the balances at unit
boundaries, then aggregates the case record only when its invariants
pass.

The forward question is to compute the steady state for an admissible
case and operating point. The learning question is to approximate a
vector of outputs from a feature vector, with recovery, log upgrade and
specific energy as separate targets. Its estimand is not simply
``accuracy'': interpolation within a known case, transfer to an entire
held-out case, and transfer to a held-out ore group define distinct
deployment questions. The optimization question chooses bounded controls
to maximize normalized recovered metal and energy subject to grade,
installed-power and water limits. The uncertainty question propagates
declared ranges in work index, head grade, liberation size and
floatability through the same engine. None of these quantities is an
economic value: OreFlow has no prices, reagent costs, capital model or
production schedule.

This formulation also marks what the product does not claim.
\(\mathcal{E}\) is steady state, not a start-up or control-loop model.
Its authored parameters are not calibrated to operating-plant
observations. An optimum is a finite-search feasible candidate from the
declared equations; it is not an operating set point. The externally
measured datasets have their own lanes and protocols. They neither alter
the authored cases nor validate their circuit parameters. These
separations are necessary to interpret every downstream score and to
avoid promoting a modeled output into a plant measurement.

The notation separates a requested setting from a solved state. The
input vector \(u\) is what the user or a study requests; the result
\(z\) is what the engine converges to. In a power-limited grind,
requested \(P_{80}\) and achieved \(P_{80}\) differ, and only the latter
belongs in a size-based energy equation. Likewise, the optimizer's
normalized decision coordinate is not a physical control until it is
mapped back through the case contract. These distinctions matter in
figures: an axis labelled with a target must not be read as an observed
outcome.

The index \(q\) denotes an evaluation protocol, not a model.
Interpolation, case holdout and grouped transfer define different
training and test sets, so \(T_q\) is retained in equations to make the
denominator explicit. The sample count \(n_q\) changes after a
rejection, while the attempted count remains fixed. A score computed
from only admitted rows is conditional on that admission rule. In the
process methods, \(i\) can index size classes and \(j\) can index
flotation cells; the corresponding vector dimensions make the intended
summation clear. Bold operators act on the size grid and are applied
separately for every mineral before stream totals are formed.

\(E\) is used for specific energy in kWh/t, while \(e\) denotes mill
energy per pass on the internal mill-feed basis. Their denominators
differ. \(M\) is recovered payable rate in t/h, not total concentrate
mass. \(G\) is grade on the stated elemental basis, not mineral mass
fraction unless the case defines it so. The report names recovery units
as percentages, and any difference in recovery is a percentage-point
difference. These conventions prevent common scale errors when comparing
kinetic percentage recovery, a mass-flow response and a dimensionless
model score.

Dimensionless normalized objectives are used only inside the optimizer;
displayed process metrics retain physical units. Recovery is reported in
percent, while the difference between two recoveries is in percentage
points. Specific energy is power divided by solids throughput and is not
interchangeable with total power. Water intensity is volume per solids
mass, whereas water flow is a volume rate. These distinctions keep
throughput changes from being mistaken for changes in process water
demand. Subscripts identify the stream, mineral, size class or sampled
state when the role is not already explicit. The conventions are carried
into schema units, serialized traces and table captions.

\section{Data and data contracts}\label{data-and-data-contracts}

The twelve case definitions are project-authored scenarios under the
repository's MIT licence, not a measured plant dataset. Each definition
identifies process family, category, scientific question, provenance,
sources, KPI ranges, nominal controls and eight variants. The committed
0.11.000 bake contains 96 variant records generated with contract digest
\texttt{0d2b65d272f3abcc6adffbf0f734c985cb002ab5cb665c109b8e8e9e31cecbd7}.
The learning design contains 3,072 simulator states, 256 per authored
case. Labels are exact relative to the implemented engine. They are not
ground truth about an operating mill.

The catalog in \texttt{docs/contracts/catalog.md} is the human-readable
authority for every contract's identifier, version, schema, producer,
consumer, missing-data behavior and status. The operating contract
supplies units, bounds, applicability and refusal rules. Case and
manifest contracts bind authored inputs and solved outputs. Benchmark
and studies contracts bind comparisons; learning, surrogate and screen
contracts bind feature order, target order, model identity, scaling and
eligibility decisions. Probe and validation contracts preserve boundary
challenges and checks. Source contracts keep the GeoMet LCT, HZDR
particle, iron-plant soft-sensor and other records distinct by
provenance and use. A missing required artifact blocks its dependent
result; it is never replaced with a default value. A failed steady state
is retained with its refusal code and denominator.

The learning data use three separate held-out protocols. Interpolation
divides rows within case; case transfer withholds all rows of a case;
group transfer withholds related ore cases together. Every scaler is fit
only on the training portion of each fold. The measured-data lanes use
source-specific records and splits. The iron-plant lane, for example,
groups consecutive hours and applies a temporal embargo; it does not
feed measurements into the circuit engine. The locked-cycle GeoMet data
lack grind, reagent and residence controls, so they cannot identify all
parameters that the model would need for calibration. Field names that
resemble operating controls do not make the missing measurements
available.

For auditability, schema validation and physical validation are
separate. JSON schemas check shape, types, required values and
enumerations. Domain gates check conservation, allowed ranges, engine
identity and cross-field invariants. Producer and consumer tests
exercise the same record from creation to use, while adversarial tests
change nested boundaries and malformed identities. A contract digest
binds the operating definition used in the bake, and each derived
artifact carries its engine version. This lets the reader distinguish a
schema-valid record from a physically accepted state and from an
independent empirical observation.

\subsection{Authored case, manifest and aggregate evidence
contracts}\label{authored-case-manifest-and-aggregate-evidence-contracts}

\label{contract-authored}

The case definition is the upstream scientific input: it binds the case
identifier, process family, descriptive question, provenance, cited KPI
ranges, nominal controls and labeled perturbations. The manifest is its
run-level companion. It identifies the engine version and
operating-contract digest, the exact artifact file, its variants,
nominal result and KPI summaries. A manifest therefore cannot be
interpreted as a new independent observation: it is an index to a named
deterministic bake. The aggregate index collects case and variant counts
and links the case-level records; consumers use it to discover the
committed suite without duplicating every stream array.

The benchmark record has a different role. It groups selected oracle
comparisons, kinetic fits, optimizer results, uncertainty outputs,
learning metrics and lane summaries under one engine identity. Its
fields summarize the scientific comparison; detailed per-case data
remain in their child records. This separation lets a reader inspect one
reported number and then follow the record that produced it. The studies
record collects the switchable studies by case, including
method-specific diagnostics and their denominators. The five artifacts
are produced by distinct pipeline stages and consumed by validation, the
web workbench, notebooks and release checks. If a case record or engine
digest is missing, the aggregate stage stops or omits only the dependent
section with an explicit status; it does not interpolate a missing case.
Version strings and digests must agree before cross-case aggregation,
because mixing engine revisions can turn a code change into apparent ore
variability.

These contracts carry authored provenance, not measurement provenance.
\texttt{case\_id}, category and family explain grouping; the localized
title and question identify the teaching problem; \texttt{sources},
\texttt{kpi\_sources} and \texttt{provenance} distinguish literature
ranges from project assumptions. \texttt{nominal} and \texttt{variants}
declare what changed between solves. The output record stores the result
and the methods tied to that setting. To extend the suite, add a new
case with explicit source or ``authored'' status, validate its operating
point against the canonical bounds, run all declared variants, and
produce the manifest from the same engine version. A benchmark summary
should be regenerated only after each child artifact passes schema and
physical checks. This prevents stale indexes from implying that every
case was recomputed.

\subsection{Operating-input and validation
contracts}\label{operating-input-and-validation-contracts}

\label{contract-operating-validation}

The operating contract declares the admissible fields, units, bounds,
case applicability and refusal behavior used by the engine and
optimizer. It is the boundary between user controls and numerical
methods. A control outside its bound is not silently clipped: the engine
returns a structured refusal that keeps the requested value and reason
visible. The nested validation checks ensure that a valid top-level
point cannot hide an invalid nested decision. Browser and Python
consumers share this declaration, and parity tests check that both
accept or refuse the same boundary values.

The contract-probe record is deliberately adversarial. It stores a
digest of the contract plus probes that test lower and upper edges,
just-outside values, wrong types, missing fields and cross-field
combinations. These probes are test evidence, not operating scenarios.
Their expected response is compared with the implementation so a UI can
distinguish a disabled control, a refused replay and a numerical result.
The validation record summarizes whether stages passed, the
stage-specific diagnostics, error list, worker count and elapsed-time
groups. A \texttt{passed} flag is an aggregate status; it does not
replace the individual errors or prove scientific validity of model
assumptions.

The validation artifact is produced after the bake and consumed by
release readiness, CI records and documentation. A missing probe or
validation file blocks the corresponding gate. A test must fail when a
required boundary changes without updating the contract; accepting a
changed schema merely because the nominal case still runs would leave
the contract drift undetected. \texttt{contract\_digest} is computed
from canonical contract content and tied into downstream artifacts. Thus
a case record with the right schema but an obsolete contract digest is
not an equivalent input to a newly declared optimizer. The reviewer
should inspect both the declared domain and the refusal evidence before
interpreting model coverage.

\subsection{Learning design and model-inference
contracts}\label{learning-design-and-model-inference-contracts}

\label{contract-learning-artifacts}

The learning design record defines feature order, target order, model
names, sample design, split identity, settings and the interpolation,
case-held-out and group-held-out results. Its fold arrays preserve
attempted, scored and rejected populations. The earlier schema version
remains declared for historical records, while the current version
carries the expanded identities and transfer diagnostics. A consumer
must not treat those versions as interchangeable: it checks the
\texttt{schema} value and the expected fields before interpreting a
score. The case group is not supplied as a predictive feature in the
transfer protocol.

The process-surrogate record binds the numerical inference transform. It
stores ordered features and targets, feature means and scales, target
means and scales, separate reconstruction-guard scaling, guard threshold
and reference ranges. These arrays are positional: changing feature
order without changing the record could produce finite but meaningless
predictions. The screen record binds the exported network and GP
metadata, covariance factor identity and the references used by the
optimizer's preflight. Inference checks model and contract identity
before evaluating a candidate. The solver uses the neural response only
to rank proposals; exact-engine evaluation remains the source of
objective and constraint values.

The learning stage produces the design and fold results; training and
export produce the model records; the notebook and workbench consume
them. Artifact hashes, engine version, contract digest, target order and
declared thresholds let the consumer reject a stale or incomplete
export. Missing scaler values, a wrong target count, non-finite model
weights or a mismatched GP factor are hard failures. They are not filled
with identity scaling or a broad interval. The frozen-model challenge
records attempted and accepted samples so guard sensitivity can be
inspected without deleting misses. Its known-case seed is a
reproducibility design, not a new-ore test. A prospective plant lane
would require a separate contract for time, sampling, assays and
production-period splits.

\subsection{External datasets and measured-source
contracts}\label{external-datasets-and-measured-source-contracts}

\label{contract-external-data}

External-data interpretation retains the original sample and source
identity, including the gravity-recovery sources and the stated particle
benchmark protocol \cite{laplante2005,laplantestaunton,pereira2021}. The
Geomet locked-cycle record holds source identity, sampling protocol and
the paired assay/recovery rows used for whole-hole and spatial-zone
evaluation. It retains which measurements are missing: grind, collector,
residence and plant circuit controls are not inferred from the assay
columns. The iron-plant soft-sensor record stores source and
data-quality descriptions, temporal split protocol, pooled scores, model
comparisons, repeated-label share, held-label definition and fold
results. The embargoed future windows and repeated laboratory values
must be read together; the raw row count is not the effective
independent sample count.

The HZDR summary declares the dataset DOI, source URL, licence, feature
groups, row counts and whether the raw file is locally available. The
particle benchmark stores the raw source identity, protocol,
standardization, fitted sparse-logistic coefficients and results per
constructed case. Test-set probabilities and author predictions are not
observed particle labels, so agreement with them is a comparison to a
constructed reference. The real-samples record preserves the source,
label meaning, assumed floatability ratios, plant or laboratory context,
comminution linkage, excluded rows, per-sample estimates and sensitivity
summaries. The GeoMet assays are not direct controls for the authored
circuit; the record therefore keeps the transformation and assumptions
explicit.

The GeoMet, HZDR, iron-plant and real-sample artifacts are produced by
separate preprocessing or evaluation stages. Their consumers include
research notebooks and the report, not the authored case generator. Each
source contract preserves its licence, stable source reference, row
exclusions and protocol boundary. A missing raw source or changed file
digest blocks reproduction of that lane while leaving independent
simulator records valid. It must not be replaced with a synthetic row
under the external-source label. Conversely, a source record that is
available and schema-valid still needs its study-specific leakage,
provenance and measurement checks before a result can be described as
measured evidence. This explicit lineage is what allows the report to
compare different evidence types without merging their denominators.

\section{System architecture}\label{system-architecture}

The pipeline is an artifact chain. Case definitions and the operating
contract enter preprocessing; the steady-state engine solves the closed
circuit and emits streams, equipment values and balance diagnostics. The
cases stage writes each nominal and variant record. The benchmark stage
compares selected results with published worked examples and retains
discrepancies. Separate stages build the particle, source-sample and
studies records. The learning stage creates a seeded design, fits the
declared models under the split protocols, and exports scalers, target
metadata, neural weights and the GP factor used by inference. Every
stage uses versioned inputs and emits its contract identity; no stage
fills a missing prerequisite by inventing a score.

Python is the research reference implementation. The TypeScript engine
is a second implementation used for the interactive workbench and is
checked against the same committed cases. That parity check detects
translation errors but is not independent validation of the physical
model, since the equations and authored constants are shared. The client
reads baked data for reported evidence and runs local calculations for
explicit user changes. The optimizer evaluates accepted candidates
through the exact process engine; the learned models may prioritize
proposals but do not supply final objective values. The app has no plant
telemetry connection.

The UI therefore exposes three value origins: a committed result, a
local engine replay, and a learned estimate. The provenance, active
case, controls and replay status accompany the relevant view. Focus mode
changes available screen area and navigation while preserving those
identities. A browser-generated state is still a result of authored
steady-state assumptions; interactive controls do not make it a plant
recommendation. This architecture is intended to make the method
inspectable: controls feed a named call, contracts constrain its domain,
artifacts bind reproducible comparisons, and the interface presents the
resulting state without implying an unrecorded source.

The artifact flow is summarized in Figure 1: versioned inputs pass
through the exact engine and stage-specific validations before reaching
the evidence records and the browser workbench. Arrows indicate handoffs
between versioned artifacts, not a live plant data feed.

A pipeline stage owns the artifact it writes, and consumers validate the
schema instead of reaching into an upstream stage's private
implementation. This arrangement makes a method replaceable without
changing the meaning of its contract. For example, a new kinetic fit may
add diagnostics to its study record, but it cannot reorder case features
or reinterpret a recovery field used by the workbench. The manifest
supplies the link from a displayed case to the exact source record,
while the contract digest ties that record to the admissible operating
domain.

The desktop and browser paths have different responsibilities. Python is
used for full scientific recomputation, model fitting and the committed
bake; TypeScript serves the interactive circuit with constrained local
state changes. A browser response may be deterministic and numerically
close to Python, but that does not make it an observation. It remains a
local calculation under the same authored assumptions. The product's API
serves versioned artifacts and health metadata; it does not proxy a
control system or receive sensor streams. Consequently, network
availability is not a model-validity test, and a successful API call is
not evidence that the nominal assumptions hold at a real site.

The release chain preserves those boundaries through version checks,
template alignment, static artifact verification, notebook execution and
the release-specific simulation bake. A candidate is not promoted
because a page renders: its engine and artifacts must first agree, and
the public bundle must then be checked against the candidate identity.
This report records the 0.11.000 release candidate and its completed
source-derived bake. Its version record is reserved under the DOI shown
on the title page. The software release remains subject to the product
repository gates, publication record synchronization and deployment
verification described below.

\section{Methods}\label{methods}

The following subsections describe the implemented methods and the
evidence they produce. They distinguish equations used to compute a
circuit response from statistical procedures used to evaluate a learned
approximation. Every comparison is conditional on the authored cases,
contract, seeds and declared acceptance criteria. Model names denote
implementations in this repository; the equations below do not imply
that their parameters were calibrated on a plant.

\subsection{Bond work-index estimate and
applicability}\label{bond-work-index-estimate-and-applicability}

\label{method-bond}

The equations are used as engineering estimates and interpreted against
the industrial work-index procedure
\cite{bond1952,gmg2021,kick1885,rittinger1867}. \begin{equation}
E_B=W_i\left(\frac{10}{\sqrt{P_{80}}}-\frac{10}{\sqrt{F_{80}}}\right).
\end{equation} \begin{equation}
W_{i,\mathrm{op}}=\frac{E_{\mathrm{op}}}{10/\sqrt{P_{80}}-10/\sqrt{F_{80}}},
\qquad \eta_B=\frac{E_B}{E_{\mathrm{op}}}.
\end{equation}

For feed and product with distributions rather than single measured
percentiles, the percentile is obtained by mass interpolation on the
documented grid. If \(F(d)\) is cumulative passing, the selected size
solves \(F(d_p)=p\). The mill balance then uses the full distribution
while the Bond diagnostic uses only \(F_{80}\) and \(P_{80}\); this
difference in information explains why their outputs need not move in
lockstep. The reported efficiency ratio is defined as
\(\eta_B=E_B/E_{op}\), with the report retaining the numerator and
denominator so that a ratio above or below one cannot hide a changed
circuit boundary. A missing percentile is a refusal, not an arbitrary
extrapolation.

The approximation is most useful for comparing a declared size reduction
under a consistent test convention. It becomes unreliable as a stand-in
for circuit simulation when feed composition, recycle, liberation or
power limit changes. The method is included because engineers recognize
it and can check the arithmetic; OreFlow's stronger process question
requires the distributional model described next.

Bond's work-index relation is applied to the solved feed and product
size distributions, rather than to a single visual setting on the
workbench. The characteristic sizes \(F_{80}\) and \(P_{80}\) are the
sizes below which eighty percent of the corresponding solids mass
passes. With sizes in micrometres and work index in the contract's
conventional units, the specific energy estimate is

\[E_B=10W_i\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{P_{80}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{F_{80}}}\right).\]

The equation is an empirical comminution relation. \(W_i\) is not a
universal ore constant inferred by OreFlow: each case carries an
authored value or a documented source, and the result inherits that
provenance. The model's purpose here is to expose the familiar
inverse-root dependence and compare it with the energy accounting of the
closed-circuit engine. It cannot resolve the full size-by-mineral
breakage distribution, recirculation or installed-power clipping by
itself. It is therefore reported next to the population-balance mill
result, never added to it as another energy contribution.

The operating work index reverses the same relation using the observed
engine energy and the solved size reduction. This makes it possible to
ask whether the simulation's net circuit energy is consistent with its
selected size range under the Bond convention. The efficiency ratio
compares the required estimate with that operating value. A ratio is
interpretable only when the reference size convention, unit conversion
and circuit boundary match; it is not a standalone equipment-efficiency
measurement. Bond's standard-circuit assumptions exclude several effects
represented elsewhere in the engine, including classification recycle
and power-limited coarse product, so discrepancy is expected when these
boundaries differ.

Rittinger's surface-area law and Kick's size-similarity law are included
as alternative functional relations. OreFlow calibrates both through the
declared reference reduction so that all three return the Bond result at
that point. Away from the reference, their curves diverge because they
encode different size dependence. This calibration is pedagogical and
does not establish that the three laws are interchangeable or that one
is universally more accurate. The meaningful comparison uses the same
feed, work index and size interval, records the reference point, and
reports divergence as a function of product size. If a case enters an
installed-power branch, an energy-only law cannot claim that the nominal
target was achieved; the solved \(P_{80}\) must be used.

The calculation uses fresh-feed tonnes as the denominator for circuit
specific energy. The mill's energy per pass is applied to its actual
circulating mill feed, which can exceed fresh feed. The report's
operating value is therefore reconstructed from mill power and
fresh-feed throughput, not copied from the per-pass quantity. Crusher
work is accounted separately with the crushing work index. These
boundaries avoid double counting and explain why a familiar Bond
estimate need not equal the complete circuit KPI. The oracle tests
compare a published worked example with explicit tolerances and preserve
any miss; agreement at one example is a calibration check on
implementation arithmetic, not universal physical validation.

For a new dataset, the method requires mass-weighted size distributions
at feed and product, a work-index test with its test protocol,
throughput, and an explicit circuit energy boundary. Sieves reported on
a volume basis must not be treated as mass fractions without a
conversion. Missing coarse or fine tails can move \(P_{80}\) materially,
so the measured size range and extrapolation rule must be stated. A
developer can call the energy-law function with the contract-normalized
sizes, then reconcile its estimate against equipment power and the
independently closed engine record. Uncertainty should vary the measured
size distribution and test-derived work index together where their
errors are correlated. A single nominal \(W_i\) and a precise calculator
output do not quantify that uncertainty.

\subsection{Size-by-mineral population balance and closed
circuits}\label{size-by-mineral-population-balance-and-closed-circuits}

\label{method-population-balance}

The size-class balance and liberation framing follow continuous
grinding, quantitative liberation and classifier treatments
\cite{herbst1980,king1979,plitt1976}. \begin{equation}
\frac{d\mathbf{m}}{dt}=(\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{D})\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{f}.
\end{equation} \begin{equation}
\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{return}}=\mathbf{C}\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{mill}},
\qquad
\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{fresh}}+\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{return}}-\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{product}}-\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{tail}}.
\end{equation}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Solve and audit a closed circuit}
\label{alg:closed-circuit}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Case contract, bounded operating point, solver tolerances
\State Validate units, bounds, mineral fractions, and circuit topology
\State Initialize recycle streams from the declared operating state
\Repeat
\State Apply breakage and unit transformations to size-by-mineral streams
\State Route classified products and update recycle streams
\State Check nonnegativity, water balance, and component mass residuals
\Until{state change and residual are within declared tolerances}
\State Return the trace only if all closure and finiteness checks pass
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The operator solve also supplies a numerical stability diagnostic. The
linear system \(A p=f\) is accepted only when its solution is finite,
nonnegative within numerical tolerance and satisfies the reconstructed
residual \(\|Ap-f\|_\infty\) below the engine tolerance. The condition
number is informative for a new size grid: near-singularity magnifies
small changes in classification return into large changes in predicted
size fractions. The current artifact does not claim a global
condition-number bound, so the refusal and residual checks are essential
safeguards.

The solved cut must also preserve the declared design load. The scalar
root function compares the generated circulating load with its target
and is bracketed over the contract interval. When no sign-changing
bracket exists, a root-finder cannot justify an extrapolated cut; the
engine should report that domain failure. Root convergence for these
scalar design equations is distinct from convergence of the mill matrix
solve. The pipeline records both outcomes so a coarse returned product
is not mistaken for a silently converged target.

The engine represents each stream by a vector over 63 size classes for
every declared mineral. Crushing and grinding are linear transformations
of these vectors for a fixed operating point, while recycles introduce a
closed-loop solve. For one mineral, let \(f\) be the fresh feed vector,
\(B\) a lower-triangular breakage operator whose columns distribute a
broken particle to finer sizes, and \(C\) a diagonal classification
return operator. The crusher product is obtained by solving the
classification and breakage relation; the mill's closed-circuit balance
has the general form

\[\left[T^{-1}(e)-C\right]p=f,\]

where \(T^{-1}(e)\) is the inverse mill transfer at energy per pass
\(e\). The matrix structure preserves mineral identity while allowing
each mineral's density, breakage and liberation attributes to alter its
path through the circuit. The operator is evaluated for each mineral and
uses the same size grid, so summing across minerals yields the solids
stream used for grade and throughput.

The breakage distribution and selection function determine different
physical effects. The breakage matrix says where mass goes after a
breakage event; the selection function describes the energy-specific
rate at which each size class breaks. Their product constructs the
breakage operator. The three perfectly mixed mill compartments use
declared volume fractions, and their combined inverse transfer is
expanded as a cubic in energy and the breakage operator. This
approximation is a specified model, not a numerical solution of a fully
resolved residence-time distribution. It is useful because it captures a
size-dependent response and remains computationally tractable for
repeated circuit solves.

Classification returns coarse material according to a partition curve.
The water bypass and corrected cut create a size-dependent return
fraction; mineral density modifies the cut so that dense particles can
report coarser than lighter particles at the same nominal size. This
feedback changes mill feed, circulating load and power. OreFlow solves
the cut and energy conditions jointly with scalar root methods. When an
installed-power constraint becomes active, it solves for an attainable
coarser product rather than reporting an impossible target. The result
records the power-limited branch so that a viewer cannot confuse a
requested target with the solved product.

For a closed circuit, the balance is a fixed point: the underflow
generated by classification returns to the mill, and the new mill
product changes the next underflow. The solver must converge both the
size distribution and scalar design conditions. A singular or
ill-conditioned transfer matrix, a circulating load above its declared
bound, or failure of the scalar bracket produces a structured refusal.
These are meaningful model-domain outcomes and are not repaired by
clipping a stream to make the arithmetic continue. Numerical tolerances
and maximum iterations are taken from the engine implementation and are
exercised by tests against balance residuals and known records.

A useful audit starts with a small hand-computable vector and verifies
that each operator preserves nonnegative mass and the total mass
expected under its declared yield. One can then compare the open-circuit
transform against the closed-loop solve as return fraction tends to
zero. In the full model, mineral-specific classification means the bulk
size distribution cannot be substituted before the loop is solved; doing
so would erase density separation. The engine therefore retains vectors
until streams have passed the unit boundaries. Only then are total
solids and elemental grades derived.

The population-balance calculation does not model transient load, liner
wear, breakage changes with time, particle-particle interactions, or a
calibrated mill survey. Each case's breakage and selection parameters
are authored or sourced with their evidence status. A sensitivity sweep
can show how a parameter changes the solved product, but it cannot turn
an assumed parameter into a measured one. Validation combines
matrix/solver checks, unit balances, published reference examples and
Python/TypeScript parity. These checks establish computational
consistency within the implemented assumptions, not fidelity to a
particular plant.

\subsection{Random-forest surrogate and case-aware
evaluation}\label{random-forest-surrogate-and-case-aware-evaluation}

\label{method-random-forest}

The reported estimator and comparison families are grounded in tree
ensembles, boosting, Gaussian-process prediction and neural fitting
\cite{breiman2001,friedman2001,rasmussen2006,loschchilov2019,hoffimann2022}.
\begin{equation}
\widehat{f}_{\mathrm{RF}}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}h_t(\mathbf{x}).
\end{equation} \begin{equation}
\operatorname{RMSE}_{\mathcal{F}}=
\sqrt{\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{F}}(y_i-\widehat{f}(\mathbf{x}_i))^2}.
\end{equation}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Fit and score a case-aware surrogate}
\label{alg:case-aware-learning}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Authored design rows, target, model configuration, held-out cases
\For{each held-out case}
\State Exclude all its rows before fitting any preprocessing
\State Fit preprocessing and model on remaining case groups
\State Predict every held-out row and apply the declared observable-range diagnostic
\If{the fold passes the diagnostic}
\State Store RMSE, MAE, and test-fold $R^2$ with the fold denominator
\Else
\State Store a rejected status and null score metrics
\EndIf
\EndFor
\State Summarize only the explicitly named admissible cohort
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The primary held-out score is computed as
\(\mathrm{RMSE}=\sqrt{n^{-1}\sum_i(y_i-\hat y_i)^2}\), where the rows
\(i\) belong only to the declared test fold. The held-out coefficient is
\(R^2=1-\mathrm{SSE}/\mathrm{SST}\), with the test-fold mean in
\(\mathrm{SST}\). It can be negative; in such a fold the predictions are
worse than the constant test-mean benchmark. Neither score is averaged
across target units. The recovery target is in percentage points, log
upgrade is dimensionless, and energy is in kWh/t.

A forest's tree spread is not used as a calibrated uncertainty interval.
Prediction error is assessed against exact engine outputs on the
reserved rows, while the frozen GP lane reports its own interval
coverage. These distinct estimands prevent an ensemble heuristic from
being mislabeled as a probability statement. The published reference
implementations run on the same feature matrix and fold boundaries; the
comparison is not copied from literature tables.

The learning lane predicts three outputs from a fixed vector of process
and ore features. The design contains 256 engine-generated operating
points per case. A row is an exact engine label for the authored circuit
state; the feature map includes normalized throughput, target size, work
index, water and mineralogical descriptors, plus circuit-presence
indicators. Case identity and group identity are not supplied as
categorical shortcuts. This design asks whether the measurable
engineered features carry transferable information, rather than whether
a model can memorize the case name.

The random forest prediction is the mean of the trees' leaf outputs.
With trees \(b=1,\dots,B\), the fitted response is

\[\hat f(x)=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}T_b(x).\]

The ensemble captures nonlinear interactions without requiring a linear
response surface, but each leaf prediction is built from observed
training targets. The finite training range therefore limits
extrapolation, and tree disagreement is not automatically a calibrated
predictive interval. OreFlow compares the forest against ridge,
histogram gradient boosting, Gaussian process and a multilayer
perceptron under identical split definitions. These alternatives
represent different inductive biases, not a search over every possible
model family.

The interpolation split retains rows from each case in training and
test. The case holdout removes all rows for one case. The ore-group
protocol removes related cases together; otherwise a sibling ore could
leak distinctive mineral properties into training and make a nominal
``new ore'' test answer a narrower interpolation question. Every fold
performs scaling inside the training partition. RMSE is computed in the
physical target units and \(R^2\) is compared with the held-out mean
baseline. Negative \(R^2\) is retained. If a fold fails the predeclared
gross-range admissibility diagnostic, its error is null, not a large
number chosen after seeing the residual.

The method's sensitivity is principally to the unit held out, the sample
design and the response scale. A random split can make an interpolation
result look strong while leaving the case or group challenge weak. Group
folds have different row counts and target variance, so an average fold
\(R^2\) is not equivalent to a pooled prediction score. For neural fits,
the saved seed outcomes expose training instability; the forest is
deterministic under its declared random state but still depends on its
sample and hyperparameters. Feature scaling, tree depth and minimum leaf
size can change fit complexity. This report uses the settings recorded
with the learning artifact and does not tune against a held-out fold.

The separate frozen-model challenge evaluates already exported artifacts
on a fresh seed within the same authored case families. That is not a
new-case transfer test. Neural outputs are inverse-scaled with the exact
released target order. An autoencoder reconstruction score is compared
with its frozen threshold, and a separate GP supplies mean and standard
deviation for interval coverage. The neural prediction is not
substituted as the GP interval centre. Controlled direct-field shifts
beyond global case bounds are synthetic probes; they test whether the
guard detects those vectors, not whether a real plant can occupy them.
Accepted shifts remain false negatives in the denominator.

For reuse with measured data, the response and feature definitions must
first be mapped to an explicit schema, with ore, production-period and time
identities retained for splitting. Replicate rows and consecutive
periods cannot cross a holdout boundary when the target is future
operation. Preprocessing is fit only on training data. Report error by
target and by held-out group, include the number of attempted, scored
and refused folds, and compare with a simple baseline. The learned lane
is an approximation to the authored engine; it is not a substitute for
exact process simulation or an empirical plant model unless new data,
calibration and prospective tests support that claim.

\subsection{Bond alternatives and energy-law
comparison}\label{bond-alternatives-and-energy-law-comparison}

\label{method-energy-laws}

The three energy-size assumptions are compared at shared inputs and a
declared common anchor \cite{bond1952,kick1885,rittinger1867,gmg2021}.
\begin{equation}
E_R=K_R\left(\frac{1}{P}-\frac{1}{F}\right),
\qquad E_K=K_K\ln\left(\frac{F}{P}\right).
\end{equation} \begin{equation}
E_B=W_i\left(\frac{10}{\sqrt{P}}-\frac{10}{\sqrt{F}}\right).
\end{equation}

Rittinger's surface-area hypothesis can be written \(E_R=K_R(1/P-1/F)\),
while Kick's size-similarity relation is \(E_K=K_K\log(F/P)\). For the
shared reference reduction \((F_r,P_r)\), the constants are set by
\(K_R=E_B(F_r,P_r)/(1/P_r-1/F_r)\) and
\(K_K=E_B(F_r,P_r)/\log(F_r/P_r)\). The fitted crossing is therefore a
chosen anchor, not an independent prediction check. These formulae
separate the assumptions: linearity in inverse size, logarithmic
similarity, and Bond's inverse square-root difference.

The equations are evaluated only for \(0<P<F\) and a positive work
index. A degenerate reduction would make the calibration denominator
zero, and the code rejects it. Away from the anchor, the ratio
\(E_R/E_B\) and \(E_K/E_B\) is the useful dimensionless comparison. This
ratio says how alternative assumptions diverge over the sampled size
range; it does not say which law is empirically best.

For a new ore, estimate \(W_i\) with a documented test and hold out size
reductions for validation. Fitting the scale and evaluating at the same
anchor is not validation. A robust comparison should include repeated
test work, uncertainty in the percentile estimates, and a held-out feed
class. OreFlow's current agreement at one worked example only verifies
implementation at that example.

The energy comparison fixes feed size, product size and work index
across Bond, Rittinger and Kick. Bond's inverse-square-root formulation
supplies the reference value at the calibrated size reduction.
Rittinger's relation makes energy proportional to newly created surface;
Kick's relation uses a logarithmic size ratio. In OreFlow, two scale
constants are selected at a declared reference pair \((F_r,P_r)\) so
that the alternative predictions intersect Bond there. Away from that
pair, the slope rather than the reference level separates the curves.
This design makes a comparison of functional assumptions visible without
pretending that the constants were independently fitted to plant tests.

The curves have an important dimensional caveat. Bond's empirical
constant carries the units needed by the chosen size convention; the
alternative constants also inherit their calibration scale. If one
changes micrometres to millimetres without transforming constants,
apparent divergence is a unit error. The code converts at the method
boundary and tests the reference equality. The report's plotted x-axis
is a solved product size, with the same feed size for every law. The
response is a law estimate, not mill power measured from equipment. When
a closed-circuit engine output is shown alongside, the reader must
account for recycle and actual mill feed before comparing it with a
fresh-feed specific-energy value.

The method does not adjudicate a universal winner. The Bond relation
embeds a conventional test and reference circuit, while Rittinger and
Kick are idealized size laws. Their divergence is informative about
model-form sensitivity. Application to coarse crushing, ultrafine
grinding, mixed mineral breakage or a power-limited mill needs evidence
that is absent from the calibration point. The right extension is a set
of size-resolved tests with replicate energy measurements and declared
apparatus boundaries. Fit the constants on a training set and hold out
an entire ore or size regime; otherwise matching the anchor point
creates a tautological ``validation.''

The code reports Bond required energy, operating work index and
efficiency ratio together. The operating index is reconstructed by
reversing the relation on the engine's actual solved size reduction; it
can differ from the authored case work index because circuit energy
includes crusher, recycle, installed-power clipping and denominator
conventions. The efficiency ratio is therefore a within-model
diagnostic. It is not an industrial plant efficiency certification. A
test report should retain throughput, measured power, size
distributions, moisture basis, and whether the denominator is fresh feed
or internal mill feed.

For a developer extending this comparison, add a candidate law behind
the same method interface, assert dimensional conversion and exact
agreement at its declared calibration point, then render its curve for
all cases without changing the existing formulas. The result artifact
should include formula identity, parameter provenance and solved sizes.
A missing or invalid size distribution should return a refusal. Do not
catch an exception and substitute zero energy, since zero would pass
into an optimizer as a highly attractive but physically meaningless
candidate.

\subsection{Five flotation kinetic forms and bank
projection}\label{five-flotation-kinetic-forms-and-bank-projection}

\label{method-kinetics}

The fitted curves and bank projection are interpreted with published
flotation kinetics, bubble-area, entrainment and particle-size evidence
\cite{polat2000,vinnett2025,gorain1997,gorain1999,savassi1998,trahar1981}.
\begin{equation}
R_1(t)=R_\infty(1-e^{-kt}),
\qquad
R_{\mathrm{fs}}(t)=R_\infty[1-f e^{-k_f t}-(1-f)e^{-k_s t}].
\end{equation} \begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{str}}(t)=R_\infty[1-e^{-(kt)^\beta}],
\qquad
R_{\mathrm{bank}}=1-\prod_{j=1}^{N}[1-R_j(\tau_j)].
\end{equation}

For a first-order batch model, the unfloated fraction is
\(U(t)=e^{-kt}\) and recovery is \(R(t)=R_\infty(1-U(t))\). The
fast/slow form mixes two survival terms,
\(U(t)=f e^{-k_f t}+(1-f)e^{-k_s t}\), with \(0\leq f\leq1\) and
nonnegative rates. A gamma distribution of rates yields a heavy-tailed
survival curve, while a compressed exponential
\(R(t)=R_\infty[1-e^{-(kt)^\beta}]\) uses shape \(\beta\) to alter early
and late response. Klimpel's form adds a rate-limiting response; each
fitted family has a different parameter interpretation and cannot be
compared solely by parameter magnitude.

The fit minimizes a declared residual objective on the common virtual
batch time grid. If \(R_i\) is the reference recovery and \(\hat R_i\)
the model value, the reported RMSE is
\(\sqrt{n^{-1}\sum_i(R_i-\hat R_i)^2}\). Bank projection then propagates
survival through each mixer with its own residence time. The batch
residual and the projected bank error are separate checks: a fit may
reproduce observed batch points yet extrapolate the late-time tail
poorly. The exact distributed-rate generator provides a known synthetic
truth but not external validation.

The kinetic comparison fits five forms to the same virtual batch record:
first order, Kelsall fast/slow, Klimpel, gamma-distributed rates and a
compressed exponential. The exact synthetic reference is generated from
a declared rate distribution and noise process. This enables a
controlled recovery of known parameters and a bank projection, but it
does not provide an independent plant test. Fits use the same time
points and objective so differences arise from model form and fitting
behavior rather than a changed dataset.

For a single first-order population, remaining floatable material
follows an exponential survival relation. A distribution of rates
averages that survival over a nonnegative rate density. A two-component
Kelsall form uses a fast fraction and slow fraction; gamma and
compressed-exponential alternatives summarize continuous heterogeneity
with fewer reported parameters. Parameter bounds enforce physically
admissible rates and fractions. The fit uses numerical optimization and
reports convergence status and residual measures; a parameter at a bound
or a flat objective may indicate that the data do not identify the
chosen form.

A batch curve is not itself the recovery of a continuous bank. OreFlow
projects the fitted survival through tanks in series using the declared
residence time per tank and flow. If a batch rate is \(k\) and each
ideal mixer has residence \(\tau\), the single-stage recovery
contribution follows the competing-rate balance, and the un-recovered
fraction compounds across the bank. Consequently, changing the number of
tanks or total residence changes the bank response even when the batch
fit is unchanged. This is a mechanistic projection under perfect-mixing
assumptions, not a measured continuous-cell calibration.

The comparison reports both batch fit error and bank projection against
the exact distributed-rate reference. A lower batch residual need not
yield a more accurate bank result: correlated parameter errors can be
amplified when survival is composed across stages. The bank projection
also makes the kinetic form's tail important. A small slow fraction can
dominate long residence, while a fast-only curve may fit early time
points but miss the late recovery. Residual plots and parameter
estimates are therefore interpreted alongside the aggregate RMSE. The
rates are not fitted to a species-specific plant database, and the
synthetic data generator's chosen truth is known to the experiment
designer.

Sensitivity is evaluated by resampling noise and changing the time
window or residence basis under a predeclared design. Such repeats
quantify variability conditional on the synthetic generator, not
between-plant variability. A batch test with sparse late samples weakly
identifies a slow component; a continuous circuit with recycle violates
the isolated-batch assumption. Feed mineralogy and liberation can also
change the effective rate distribution. The method should not
extrapolate beyond the tested concentration, aeration and reagent domain
merely because an exponential curve remains mathematically defined.

With new data, preserve replicate test identity, sampling time, assay
error, initial mass, concentrate and tail mass, and residence-time
definition. Close species balances first. Fit every kinetic form on the
same training replicates, reserve whole tests for evaluation, and
project all fitted curves through the same bank geometry. Report
convergence, parameter-bound hits, batch residuals, endpoint recovery,
bank error and the mass-balance closure. Distinguish water recovery from
valuable-mineral recovery and identify whether a record is batch,
continuous-cell or modeled. These checks allow the method comparison to
be extended without mixing different physical estimands.

\subsection{Constrained pattern search and guarded
proposals}\label{constrained-pattern-search-and-guarded-proposals}

\label{method-optimization}

The baseline mesh search, progressive barrier and surrogate safeguards
are compared with their stated derivative-free optimization frameworks
\cite{torczon1997,audet2006,audet2009,booker1999}. \begin{equation}
\max_{\mathbf{x}\in[\mathbf{l},\mathbf{u}]}J(\mathbf{x})
\quad\text{subject to}\quad
g_j(\mathbf{x})\leq0,\quad h_k(\mathbf{x})=0.
\end{equation} \begin{equation}
\mathbf{x}_{n+1}\in\{\mathbf{x}_n+\Delta_n\mathbf{d}:\mathbf{d}\in D_n\},
\qquad
\text{an exact engine result is required before an incumbent update}.
\end{equation}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Guard a surrogate proposal with exact evaluation}
\label{alg:guarded-search}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Feasible incumbent, mesh, learned guard, exact circuit engine
\State Generate bounded poll and mesh-neighbour candidates
\State Rank eligible candidates using the learned objective estimate
\For{each selected proposal}
\State Apply reconstruction and uncertainty-width guards
\If{both guards pass}
\State Evaluate the proposal with the exact process engine
\If{the result is finite, feasible under the barrier, and improves the incumbent}
\State Update incumbent and record the exact result
\EndIf
\EndIf
\EndFor
\State Retain ordinary exact polling and record calls, refusals, and termination
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The search's reported finite result depends on initialization, mesh,
coordinate order, constraint tolerance and evaluation budget. Each
decision is mapped to \([0,1]\) using its contract bounds. Poll points
outside the cube are never evaluated. A candidate with engine status
refused cannot update either incumbent. For a valid evaluation, the
objective is compared only after checking status and finite values. This
explicit status handling prevents a failure, NaN or clipped metric from
becoming a spurious optimum.

The progressive barrier tracks the best feasible objective and a
least-violating infeasible incumbent. Feasible dominance is
objective-based; infeasible progress is ordered by violation and
objective under the current barrier. The threshold is nonincreasing, so
an infeasible incumbent cannot become acceptable by relaxing the
criterion after the fact. The implementation's convergence status is
tied to its own stop rule. Classical pattern-search convergence theorems
require assumptions on continuity, polling directions and refinement
that the learned proposal insertion does not automatically establish;
this report claims observed finite-suite behavior only.

A useful method ablation disables the reconstruction guard and the
GP-width threshold separately, while keeping proposals, objective,
starts and budget fixed. It should report exact calls, rejected
candidates, endpoint feasibility and same-start differences, with
repetitions across declared cases. The existing 72-pair evidence tests
the integrated screen against the unscreened baseline and gives a
negative compute result; it does not identify which filter component
caused the extra calls.

The optimization method is a generalized pattern search with a
progressive feasibility barrier. Its decision vector is normalized to
the unit cube of the case-specific operating contract. At each mesh,
fixed coordinate directions generate trial points around the feasible
and least-violating incumbents. The process engine evaluates
objective and constraints. Contract-bound violations are rejected by an
extreme barrier. Grade, power and water remain relaxable process
constraints whose violations are aggregated as

\[h(u)=\sum_{k}\max(0,-s_k(u)-\epsilon)^2,\]

where \(s_k\) is a normalized constraint slack and \(\epsilon\) is the
declared feasibility tolerance. Feasibility and objective improvement
are separate: an infeasible point can reduce violation without becoming
the best feasible solution.

Each start begins with mesh \(\Delta=1/4\). A dominating trial may
expand or retain the mesh according to the recorded outcome; an
unsuccessful poll contracts it. The search stops at the minimum mesh or
at its distinct-evaluation budget, with a reason stored in the result. A
budget stop is not convergence. Up to six starts are evaluated and
duplicate starts are removed. The engine reevaluates the selected
terminal point from a fresh state before it is reported. If no feasible
start exists, the record retains an infeasible status and the
least-violating point rather than fabricating an optimum. The objective
weights recovered payable metal and specific energy relative to the base
state; the baked primary result uses the declared metal-only weight and
companion weight-path records are separate.

The screen proposes candidates one or two mesh steps from incumbents. A
frozen neural model predicts their outputs, an autoencoder guard rejects
features outside its learned reconstruction threshold, and a Gaussian
process rejects candidates whose recovery half-width exceeds the
configured limit. Passing candidates are ranked by the neural objective,
then the exact engine performs the evaluation. The GP mean is not
substituted for the neural prediction, and the interval is not claimed
to be calibrated plant uncertainty. The normal pattern poll remains
available. This arrangement preserves exact objective values but does
not by itself prove convergence of the modified finite implementation.

The experiment compares paired searches with the same case, starts,
objective, bounds, tolerances and budget, differing only by screening.
Distinct cached trial-state counters measure the declared workload; they
exclude model fitting, inference latency, uncached audits and the later
weight path. The observed 72-variant suite has 26,367 screened versus
22,487 unscreened recorded evaluations, a 17.25\% increase; counts rise
in 68 variants, tie in four, and fall in none. Thus the negative result
directly refutes a compute-saving claim for this suite and measure. It
does not prove that every screen increases runtime or calls.

Decision agreement is reported separately from work. Both searches
return feasible endpoints in 70 cases and infeasible outcomes in two;
terminal outcomes match in 65. The seven differing feasible decisions
have small recovered-metal differences, yet close endpoints do not
compensate for added exact work. The proposal record includes 5,811
exact evaluations, of which 901 improve the incumbent, and maximum
absolute recovery error of 38.158 percentage points. That maximum
demonstrates that the uncertainty-width rule is not a universal error
guarantee. These outcomes motivate ablations by guard and interval
threshold, but any new threshold must be fixed a priori for a new paired
run.

A new application should freeze case identity, starting points, decision
bounds, objective weights, exact-call accounting and stopping rules
before comparison. Store every proposal, evaluated or not, its
prediction and exact outcome when available, guard decision, slacks, and
termination status. Compare paired counts and endpoint feasibility, and
instrument wall time separately. A model that rejects an invalid plant
state should not be credited with savings unless those rejected calls
would otherwise have reached the exact engine. The operating endpoint
must be reevaluated and reviewed by the responsible process team before
any plant trial; this research optimizer is not an operational
controller.

\subsection{Latin-hypercube propagation and Sobol
decomposition}\label{latin-hypercube-propagation-and-sobol-decomposition}

\label{method-sobol}

The sampling and estimator follow variance-based sensitivity analysis;
the separate geomet uncertainty study motivates retaining spatial-group
structure \cite{saltelli2010,herman2017,hoffimann2022}. \begin{equation}
S_i=\frac{\operatorname{Var}_{X_i}(\operatorname{E}[Y\mid X_i])}{\operatorname{Var}(Y)},
\qquad
S_{T_i}=1-\frac{\operatorname{Var}_{X_{\sim i}}(\operatorname{E}[Y\mid X_{\sim i}])}{\operatorname{Var}(Y)}.
\end{equation} \begin{equation}
\widehat{S}_{T_i}=
\frac{\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}(Y_{A,n}-Y_{AB_i,n})^2}{\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(Y)}.
\end{equation}

The sensitivity estimator is meaningful only for independent factors and
a nonconstant output over the specified box. If the total-order estimate
exceeds the first-order estimate by more than its numerical tolerance,
the difference is interpreted as interaction contribution, but
finite-sample estimators can reverse that ordering slightly. If the
response variance is effectively zero, the ratio is undefined in
practice and the method reports a constant response rather than
assigning a high index to floating-point noise. The bootstrap is
retained as a conservative sampling diagnostic; a low-discrepancy design
violates the bootstrap's independent-row premise, so the reported
half-width is not a calibrated 95\% interval.

A convergence study should vary both the base sample count and seed,
compare index ranks and uncertainty widths, and track refused draws.
Increasing \(N\) multiplies the engine budget by \(D+2\), so a
higher-resolution result has a declared computational cost. The current
baked \(N=256\) and stored bootstrap design define the values displayed
in the application. A user-triggered seed or sample-count replay is a
separate calculation and must not replace the committed result without
creating a new artifact and identity.

The uncertainty lane perturbs four inputs around each case's nominal
operating point: work index, head grade, liberation size and, for
flotation cases, floatability. The declared half-width is uniform and
independent across the sampled factors. A seeded Latin hypercube
stratifies each marginal into \(N\) bins and applies a randomized
permutation. This provides broad marginal coverage with fewer runs than
unstructured Monte Carlo for the same sample count, while retaining
reproducible finite-sample design. It does not encode correlations among
ore properties, because a dependence model is not present in the
contract.

For each admitted row, the exact engine recomputes a circuit state. The
artifact stores the sample inputs, seed, solved/refused identity, output
vector, quantiles, mean, standard deviation and constraint
probabilities. Refused draws are excluded from solved-output moments but
count as failures in feasibility probabilities. This denominator split
is necessary: the distribution of the outputs conditional on a steady
state is not the distribution of all attempted inputs. A chart showing
only solved responses without the attempted count would conceal the most
important domain boundary.

The sensitivity study uses Saltelli's variance-based design with base
matrices \(A\) and \(B\) and one hybrid matrix per input. For response
\(Y=f(X)\), the first-order and total-effect estimators are

\[\hat S_i=\frac{N^{-1}\sum_j f(B)_j\{f(A_B^{(i)})_j-f(A)_j\}}{\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(Y)},\qquad
\hat S_{T_i}=\frac{(2N)^{-1}\sum_j\{f(A)_j-f(A_B^{(i)})_j\}^2}{\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(Y)}.\]

The design costs \(N(D+2)\) evaluations for \(D\) inputs. OreFlow uses a
scrambled Sobol sequence and bootstrap estimates from SALib. These
resampled-row half-widths are conservative diagnostics for this
low-discrepancy design, not calibrated confidence intervals; a separate
validation on analytic test functions found substantial overcoverage.
Small negative estimates and total effects slightly below first-order
estimates are sampling artifacts, not negative physical contributions.
When response variance is below the declared constant-output tolerance,
the record says the output is constant instead of dividing numerical
noise by numerical noise.

If any Saltelli row is refused, the full matrix cannot be evaluated
consistently; the artifact records the refusal and omits indices. This
is not equivalent to dropping that draw and recomputing the estimator.
The same rule applies in the browser replay. A structural check is
available: energy should not depend on floatability when that input does
not enter the energy calculation, so its effect should be exactly zero
under the paired design. Such negative controls reveal accidental
dependencies or a variable-order error.

Interpretation is bounded by the four selected ranges, independence
assumption, sample count and steady-state solver. A high total index
means the output varies strongly over this designed box; it is not a
universal ranking over all deposits. To reuse the method, define
supported input ranges and any dependencies before generating the
design, keep refused rows, use exact engine outputs, and report
attempted and solved denominators. Increase sample size as a convergence
study rather than relying on one seed. If domain knowledge requires
correlated inputs, use a method that preserves the joint distribution
and document how its estimands differ from the independent Sobol
decomposition.

\section{Process model}\label{process-model}

The record reproduces all 96 committed states within 1e-6 in the browser
port and all 96 committed states close within 1e-9 at every unit in the
Python engine. The port shares constants and is a translation check, not
an independent validation of physical assumptions.

\subsection{Representation}\label{representation}

Every stream holds, for each mineral, a vector of mass flows (t/h) over
one grid of 63 size classes on a fourth-root-of-two progression from 150
mm to a pan below 3.24 µm, and a water flow. Assays are computed from
mineral masses and the minerals' element contents (from their formulas
and the IUPAC atomic weights); no grade is stored, so a grade changes
only when a mass does. Liberation is described per valuable mineral by
size (King 1979): a liberated fraction, and composites whose content and
host are declared.

Each stream is represented by nonnegative mass rates indexed by mineral
and size class, alongside a separate water rate. The representation
retains class fractions until all units in a circuit have been
evaluated; converting to a single bulk size or head grade before
classification would erase mineral-specific partitioning. Mineral assays
are derived from elemental composition and mineral fractions after the
mass streams close. Every input grid is ordered and checked for
duplicate or invalid class edges. Fractions are normalized only where
the contract explicitly defines a distribution, and an invalid sum is
rejected with the field path. This makes the state suitable for both
physical balance equations and downstream comparison of per-mineral size
curves.

\subsection{Comminution}\label{comminution}

The crusher is the Whiten form (Duarte et al.~2021) \cite{duarte2021},
with a strictly lower-triangular breakage matrix \(\mathbf{B}\) and a
classification function \(\mathbf{C}\) set by the closed-side setting:

\[
\mathbf{p}=(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{C})(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{B}\mathbf{C})^{-1}\mathbf{f}
\]

The ball mill is an energy-specific population balance (Herbst and
Fuerstenau 1980) in the form of the Moly-Cop tools \cite{molycoptools}:
three perfect mixers of volume fractions 0.70, 0.15 and 0.15 share the
breakage operator
\(\mathbf{D}=(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{B})\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{S}^{E})\),
so the mill's inverse transfer at an energy per pass \(e\) is a cubic
polynomial in \(\mathbf{D}\), and the closed circuit with the cyclone
returning a fraction \(C_i\) of each class solves

\[
\begin{aligned}
\left[\mathbf{T}^{-1}(e)-\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{C})\right]\mathbf{p}
  &=\mathbf{f},\\
\mathbf{T}^{-1}(e)
  &=\mathbf{I}+c_1e\mathbf{D}+c_2e^2\mathbf{D}^2+c_3e^3\mathbf{D}^3
\end{aligned}
\]

per mineral. Two scalar unknowns, the energy per pass that meets the
target P80 and the host cut that holds the design circulating load, are
found by the Illinois method. The energy per pass is per tonne of mill
feed, so the mill draws \(e\,m\), with \(m\) the fresh feed plus the
cyclone underflow (less what a gravity bleed takes from it); when that
exceeds the installed power, the circuit runs at installed power,
\(e\,m(e)=P_{\mathrm{inst}}\) solved as a fixed point, with a coarser
product, and says so. The reported grinding energy is that net energy
per tonne of fresh feed, \(\frac{e\,m}{F}\); the crushing energy is
Bond's with the crushing work index, and the Bond requirement
\(E=10W_i\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{P}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{F}}\right)\) gives the
operating work index and the efficiency ratio beside it, with Rittinger
and Kick calibrated to agree with Bond at a reference reduction and
never added to it (Bond 1952; GMG 2021).

\subsection{Classification and
separation}\label{classification-and-separation}

Hydrocyclones follow Plitt (1976): a Rosin-Rammler partition

\[
y(d)=R_f+(1-R_f)\left[1-\exp\!\left(-\ln 2\left(\frac{d}{d_{50c}}\right)^m\right)\right]
\]

with a water bypass \(R_f\) from the water balance and a cut corrected
for each mineral's density, so dense minerals classify as coarser
particles and circulate. Plitt's sizing of the cyclone cluster is
uncalibrated and fails its published check (section 3), so the cluster's
count and pressure are reported as an estimate, never as a result.
Flotation banks are perfect mixers in series; the rate constant of each
particle class follows from the bubble surface area flux
\(S_b=\frac{6J_g}{D_{32}}\) (Gorain et al.~1997, 1999) and a size window
(Trahar 1981), a composite floats at the liberated rate scaled by its
exposed surface, \(P_{\mathrm{comp}}=P_V\phi^{\frac{2}{3}}\) with
\(\phi\) the valuable mineral's volume share, entrainment follows
Savassi et al.~(1998), and cleaner and recleaner tails return to the
preceding bank. Each cell's residence is its pulp volume, less the gas
hold-up, over the flow that leaves it as tail, which depends on what the
cell floats, so it is solved per cell as a fixed point:

\[
\begin{aligned}
r_j&=\frac{k\tau_j+\mathrm{ENT}\,w_j}{1+k\tau_j+\mathrm{ENT}\,w_j},\\
\tau_j&=\frac{V(1-\varepsilon_g)}{Q_{\mathrm{tail},j}},\\
R_N&=1-\prod_j(1-r_j)
\end{aligned}
\]

Gold follows the gravity-recoverable gold (GRG) model of Laplante,
Woodcock and Noaparast as Vincent (1997) states it: the gold is a GRG
component with its own size distribution, from a GRG test, plus a
non-GRG component; the GRG's cyclone cut takes a density correction with
an exponent of 1.0, fitted to measured GRG partitions where Stokes would
give 0.5; the gravity unit on a bleed of the cyclone underflow, or of
the mill discharge, recovers GRG by size at a per-pass recovery
\(R(d)=R_{\max}\left[1-\exp\!\left(-\left(\frac{d}{x_g}\right)^2\right)\right]\);
and grinding moves GRG to non-GRG at a slowed breakage rate (Banisi et
al.). Low-intensity magnetic drums capture liberated magnetite and
composites by their magnetite content; desliming cyclones discard slimes
ahead of flotation, with the water split following the solids sent down
at a declared underflow density.

\subsection{Balance closure and the
contract}\label{balance-closure-and-the-contract}

An independent recomputation calculates, from the output streams alone,
the closure of every mineral, every species and the water at every unit
and for the circuit, and of every size class at the units that do not
break particles. The operating point is thirteen inputs declared once,
with units, per-case bounds, steps, the families they apply to, a
cross-field rule and bilingual messages; the exported declaration is
interpreted identically by the engine's validator, the web service and
the browser, so a state is accepted or rejected with the same code
everywhere. A state the engine cannot solve, such as a classifier cut at
which the mill cannot draw its installed power, is refused with a reason
rather than answered.

The engine records residuals before reducing the trace to headline KPIs.
For each mineral, fresh input equals the sum of product, tail and
declared inventory change at the modeled steady state; water is checked
separately because its units and routing differ. The contract specifies
relative and absolute tolerances so small streams are not judged only
against a large plant-wide denominator. A refusal preserves the failing
unit, residual and reason in the run record. The report evaluates
closure at internal boundaries and for the complete circuit, which can
detect a duplicated recycle even when the final aggregate mass appears
plausible. Closure verifies the implementation's accounting identity,
not the accuracy of authored process parameters.

\section{Experimental protocol}\label{experimental-protocol}

\subsection{Authored circuits}\label{authored-circuits}

Twelve authored cases in four circuit families: nine copper, nickel,
zinc and refractory-gold rougher-cleaner flotation circuits
(\texttt{rougher}), free-milling gold with a gravity bleed
(\texttt{gravity\_rougher}), fine magnetite with magnetic drums instead
of flotation (\texttt{magnetic}), and phosphate with desliming ahead of
flotation (\texttt{deslime\_rougher}). Every parameter carries its unit
and either a source or the label authored; none is fitted to a plant.
Five of the twelve are copper sulphide plants on chalcopyrite, and the
two gold plants carry most of their gold in pyrite (55\%, beside
electrum in the free-milling ore and arsenopyrite in the refractory
one); the other five each have a payable and dominant carrier no other
case shares.

Cases cover rougher flotation, magnetic separation, gravity recovery,
leaching and desliming configurations represented by the engine. Their
identifiers, provenance notes, mineralogy and equipment declarations are
preserved in each case record. The nominal operating point is not
treated as a measured baseline: it is an authored reference state for
controlled perturbations. Variant definitions change one named operating
or ore factor at a time where the contract permits, while classifier-cut
modes remain separate when their input features are outside the learned
screen. The case table reports the number of variants and the response
quantities available for each family, so an absent KPI is not read as a
zero. Group labels are retained for transfer evaluation but are withheld
from the model features.

\subsection{Design, features and
targets}\label{design-features-and-targets}

For each case a scrambled Sobol sequence draws 256 states the contract
accepts over the case's whole envelope and two ore factors (liberation
size and floatability within their uncertainty half-widths), except that
magnetite varies liberation alone. There are 3072 states in all; the
engine simulates every state. Each state is described by 22 physical
features computed before simulation: the payable fraction, throughput
per installed megawatt, the grind, circulating load, water, crusher
setting and work index, the payable carriers' share-weighted liberation
size, composite content, density, floatability and dose ratio, the
rougher gas velocity, cells and volume per t/h, the gravity bleed, the
desliming cut and four circuit descriptors. The case identity is never a
feature. The targets are recovery (\%), the log10 upgrade ratio
(concentrate over head grade) and the total specific energy (kWh/t).

The design contains 3,072 simulated states, allocated across twelve
cases and generated from a versioned space-filling plan. Each row
retains its case, ore group and source variant outside the predictor
matrix so that grouping remains possible without leaking the identity
into training. Features describe feed composition, size and liberation,
hardness, water and operating controls; targets are trained separately
because recovery, log upgrade and specific energy have different units
and error meanings. The stored design seed and feature order are part of
the artifact identity. Duplicate states are audited before splitting.
These details permit another researcher to reconstruct the training
table and detect changes in case balance, target scaling or group
membership.

\subsection{Models and protocols}\label{models-and-protocols}

Ridge (alpha 1); a random forest (300 trees; Breiman 2001); histogram
gradient boosting (300 iterations; Friedman 2001); a Gaussian process
with a squared-exponential kernel with one length scale per feature and
a noise term, on a seeded subsample of 500 training rows (Rasmussen and
Williams 2006); and a perceptron of two hidden layers of 64 units,
trained by full-batch AdamW with early stopping on a 15\% validation
split (Loshchilov and Hutter 2019). Features are standardized on the
training rows of each split. Perceptron targets are standardized on
those same rows; the Gaussian process normalizes its own training
responses, while ridge and the tree models fit targets in their declared
units. Because the Gaussian process sees fewer rows, ridge, random
forest and gradient boosting are also refitted on the same 500 rows for
a separate comparison. The perceptron's ordinary scores retain the full
training subset. Because one training seed is one draw, the perceptron
is retrained with four more seeds for interpolation and each of the
twelve case holdouts. The two multi-case ore-group fits use the base
seed; no five-seed group-transfer stability claim follows from this
study.

The standardization fits the training subset alone. Numerically constant
training columns retain unit scale, including columns whose small
positive variance comes from summation roundoff. An unseen value is
therefore not amplified by division through a spurious near-zero scale.
Neither held-out features nor held-out targets determine the fitted
transformation. The interpolation comparison also retains a case-mean
baseline fitted on each case's training rows, and an R² denominator
based on within-case variation, separating case recognition from
response prediction.

\emph{Interpolation} holds out 20\% of the states inside every case
(2460 training, 612 test rows). \emph{Leave one case out} holds out each
case in turn; the models that predict a case never saw any of its
states, but they may have seen plants on the same ore. \emph{Leave one
ore group out} holds out every plant that shares the held-out plant's
payable and dominant carrier mineral: the five chalcopyrite plants
together (1792 training, 1280 test rows), the two gold plants together
(2560 training, 512 test rows), and each of the other five alone, where
it is the same fold as leaving one case out; seven groups in all. An
autoencoder (16, 6 and 16 units) trained on the features gives a guard:
its threshold is the 99th percentile of the reconstruction errors of its
validation rows. Its false-alarm rate is measured on the 612 held-out
in-envelope states. Its headline false-accept rate uses 11,016 probes,
each a held-out state pushed half a training span above the maximum of
one continuous feature. Separate acceptance curves repeat those probes
at six distances from 0.02 to 1.0 spans, independently above the maximum
and below the minimum.

\subsection{Prediction diagnostics and admissible
comparisons}\label{prediction-diagnostics-and-admissible-comparisons}

Shape and finiteness are checked before an error is computed. A further
gross-range diagnostic compares the prediction range with the target
range over the complete authored design, expanded by one observed span
on each side. For reference limits \(a\) and \(b\), the diagnostic
interval is

\[[a-(b-a),\ b+(b-a)].\]

This is a post-inference diagnostic and includes held-out target values.
It is not a training-support test, an operational constraint, an
independent transfer validation or a rule for selecting fitted
parameters. No prediction is clipped or refitted using these limits.
Because the test concerns disjoint ranges, it can miss extreme
individual predictions when other predictions overlap the interval;
passing it does not establish physical validity at every state.

A finite transfer forecast wholly outside that interval is retained as a
rejected transfer, with null RMSE, MAE and R², unmodified
observed/predicted/reference ranges, affected-row count and model,
target, protocol, fold and seed identifiers. The same check applies to
every constituent case of a grouped fold, so pooled overlap cannot
conceal an entirely invalid case. Nonfinite, shape, fitting and
programming failures abort evaluation; interpolation range failures also
abort. Finite poor forecasts inside the diagnostic envelope retain their
errors and negative R². Unexpected constant forecasts are identified
separately from the intentionally constant baseline.

Conditional summaries report attempted, scored and rejected counts
beside their error statistics. A missing admissible cohort has null
metrics. Comparisons across models additionally use the intersection of
folds admissible for every model, with all excluded identifiers
retained. These matched summaries remain conditional descriptions of
that intersection; they do not erase a model's failed transfers or rank
performance over the complete planned design. Complete per-fold,
per-case and per-seed evidence is retained in the release record.

\subsection{Energy and upgrade
targets}\label{energy-and-upgrade-targets}

The one-case-out median R² for total specific energy ranges from 0.652
(ridge) to 0.952 (gradient boosting); under ore-group holdout it ranges
from 0.617 (ridge) to 0.907 (Gaussian process). Log-upgrade median R² is
negative for every model under group holdout, so the recovery transfer
result does not generalize to grade-upgrade prediction. The held-out
protocols and target definitions remain separate in the record.

Specific energy is measured in kilowatt-hours per tonne of solids at the
declared circuit boundary. The upgrade target is the logarithm of
concentrate grade divided by feed grade, which reduces the scale
difference between low- and high-grade cases but changes the error unit
to a dimensionless log ratio. Predictions are inverse-transformed before
computing grade-scale diagnostics; averaging log errors is not
equivalent to averaging percentage-point errors. Recovery remains an
independent target because it is a valuable-mineral fraction and does
not determine grade without mass pull. The separate heads prevent an
apparently good composite score from hiding a severe error in one
process objective. All three target arrays remain paired with the
identical held-out rows.

\subsection{Interval coverage}\label{interval-coverage}

Nominal 95\% Gaussian-process recovery intervals cover 90.7\% of
interpolation rows and 82.9\% of pooled case-held-out rows. The worst
case-fold coverage is 35.5\%, and four case folds cover fewer than 80\%
of their rows. These intervals use the GP's own mean and predictive
standard deviation. Coverage is target- and split-specific; the
authored-plant split does not establish calibrated intervals on real
plants.

Coverage is computed from the held-out reference falling between the
model's lower and upper predictive quantiles, divided by the number of
scored rows in that fold. The nominal level is a design target, not a
guarantee. Coverage should be read beside interval width: intervals can
cover nearly every point by being too broad to inform a decision. The
report uses interpolation coverage as a diagnostic and separately shows
transfer-fold behavior; pooling those records would obscure changes in
the test distribution. Rejected folds do not contribute to scored-row
coverage and remain visible in the attempted/scored/rejected
denominator. A calibration procedure would need a prospective split and
recalibration data not used to choose the model or its guard.

\subsection{Reconstruction guard}\label{reconstruction-guard}

On the held-out interpolation experiment, the autoencoder flags 1.47\%
of 612 in-envelope states and accepts 15.51\% of 11,016 upward-shift
probes at half a training span. Its per-feature and per-distance
acceptance rates remain in \texttt{learning.json}. This validation guard
differs from the final guard refitted for deployment. A low
reconstruction error is a screening signal, not a process constraint, a
guarantee of physical safety or a substitute for the operating contract.

The reconstruction guard compares a candidate feature vector with the
learned model's training representation and applies a threshold frozen
from the declared calibration records. The guard is a support diagnostic
rather than a calibrated probability of correctness. It can accept an
unusual vector if reconstruction error is low, or reject a valid vector
if the representation cannot encode a legitimate process combination.
The direct-shift probes deliberately vary individual fields beyond
global case ranges to measure missed detections under controlled
perturbations; they are not observed plant states. The full record
identifies shifted feature, magnitude, threshold, decision and exact
engine response where one was requested. A new deployment would require
prospective challenge cases and a documented false-acceptance cost.

\section{Kinetic, optimization and sensitivity
studies}\label{kinetic-optimization-and-sensitivity-studies}

The engine produces kinetic, optimization and sensitivity records for
each variant. Kinetic fits, sensitivity and unscreened optimization use
the mechanistic engine. Screened optimization additionally consumes the
exported neural surrogate, reconstruction guard and Gaussian-process
interval.

For the paired search comparison, the recorded evaluation counter counts
distinct trial states in each fixed-weight multistart search's cache; a
state shared across starts is counted once. Screened and unscreened runs
use separate caches, and refused trial outcomes remain recorded. These
counters are captured before the subsequent warm-started weight-path
study. They exclude uncached base/endpoint audits, model training and
screening inference, so they are a bounded search-accounting measure
rather than a tally of every simulator invocation or a wall-clock
benchmark.

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Kinetic lumping.} Five lumped models fitted to a virtual batch
  test of the rougher feed, projected to the bank under the same
  residence distribution and compared with the exact bank recovery by
  true flotation, over 88 flotation states: mean absolute projection
  errors of 1.27 points for the gamma model, 1.81 for Kelsall, 2.80 for
  Klimpel, 4.76 for the stretched exponential and 7.56 for first order
  (Polat and Chander 2000; Vinnett and Waters 2025). The first-order
  projection falls below the exact result at every nominal state: a
  batch curve that is a mixture of rates is not one exponential.
\item
  \textbf{Constrained optimization.} A generalized pattern search with a
  progressive barrier from six starts (Torczon 1997; Audet and Dennis
  2006, 2009) finds a point within the grade, power and water
  constraints for 94 of the 96 variants; the two exceptions are the
  magnetite circuit's harder ore and higher throughput, where the grind
  is the only decision and the mill is already at installed power. The
  learned lane screens the search step (Booker et al.~1999): candidates
  are ranked by the perceptron's prediction and reach the engine only
  where the guard accepts their state and the Gaussian process's 95\%
  half-width on recovery is within 5 points, a novelty measure for the
  perceptron rather than an interval on what it ranks; the poll and the
  optimum stay engine results. The screen did not save engine work: over
  the 72 screened variants it used 26,367 engine evaluations versus
  22,487 without screening, 17.25\% more. It saved calls in none, used
  more in 68 variants and tied in four. Mean absolute recovery error
  averaged 0.66 percentage points across the 68 variants with evaluated
  proposals; four variants have no proposal-error estimate rather than
  zero error. Across 5,811 evaluated proposals its mean was 0.71 points,
  median 0.48 and maximum 38.16. Both procedures had the same terminal
  outcome in 65 variants: 63 identical feasible decisions and two
  matching infeasible outcomes. Seven feasible pairs selected different
  decisions, with recovered-metal differences no greater than 0.002653
  t/h (0.017\%). Five favored the unscreened decision and two the
  screened decision.
\item
  \textbf{Sensitivity.} Sobol indices of four uncertain ore properties
  at each nominal state (Saltelli et al.~2010; Herman and Usher 2017):
  floatability drives recovery in ten cases, liberation size drives
  concentrate grade in seven and the head grade in the other five, the
  work index drives grinding energy and the head grade drives recovered
  metal in all twelve.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}\label{discussion}

The test bounds transfer between authored plants. A thirteenth authored
plant is not a real one: the engine is a steady-state model with
authored parameters, no froth stability, no dynamics and no prices, and
its twelve plants share its structural assumptions. That is a feature of
the design, since the teacher's answer is exact and reproducible, and a
limit of the conclusion, since a real plant can depart from all twelve
at once.

Four interpretation limits emerge from this simulator study. First,
interpolation and transfer answer different questions: a good
interpolation score coexists with a rejected transfer and a large finite
error under another seed. Second, the held-out unit changes the
evidence. Sharing a dominant mineral does not make whole-case and
whole-group tests interchangeable; the two gold cases expose a large
grouped error. Third, admissibility diagnostics change the scored
denominator. A lower conditional mean cannot be presented as an
improvement over a complete-design mean, and even a matched comparison
remains conditional on the shared admissible folds. Fourth,
reconstruction errors miss some controlled feature shifts, so their
acceptance rates need the feature, distance and tested population beside
them. These observations motivate a measured study on real operating
periods; they do not prove the same rankings or rates on a plant.

A calibrated study would replace the authored targets by measured
metallurgy across operating periods, calibrate the engine on some
periods and hold out an entire period and an ore family, and score both
point error and whether the surrogate preserves the ranking of operating
decisions.

The strongest product result is reproducibility of a declared
computational experiment, not generalization to a mine. A closed balance
is necessary for a meaningful simulator response, but mass closure alone
cannot identify a wrong breakage law, liberation curve or floatability
parameter. Similarly, close Python and browser outputs show that two
implementations agree on the same equations and inputs; they can share
the same structural error. The measured-data lane shows this distinction
in practice: the engine has a sizeable recovery bias against GeoMet
samples, while data-lane regressors reduce that particular error under a
different target and feature contract. Those regressors do not repair
the mechanistic model because they estimate from the external data lane
and cannot be substituted for the circuit equations. The negative screen
result is also useful: it rules out a claimed call saving under the
evaluated finite settings and motivates targeted ablations before
further claims.

\section{Limitations}\label{limitations}

OreFlow represents a steady-state, deterministic flowsheet with authored
equipment and ore parameters. It does not model plant transients, sensor
delay, control logic, froth stability, stochastic equipment failure,
maintenance, reagent cost, energy tariffs, concentrate penalties, water
scarcity prices, capital expenditure or mine scheduling. An optimized
decision in this engine therefore maximizes a declared simulator
objective and satisfies declared simulator constraints; it is not an
economic optimum or an operating recommendation.

The twelve cases are designed research scenarios, not a probability
sample of mines. Their shared equations and parameterization make
within-engine experiments reproducible, but create structural dependence
among cases. The 3,072 learning states come from a designed simulator
grid, so even whole-case and ore-group partitions test transfer within
that authored domain. The measured GeoMet samples come from a different
data lane and show a substantial mechanistic recovery bias; the missing
plant controls, equipment states and process-time alignment prevent
attributing that bias to one physical cause. The particle reference has
constructed test probabilities rather than observed liberation labels.
Neither source supplies prospective validation on a production plant.

The uncertainty calculations vary declared factors under an
independent-factor design. They do not encode geological covariance,
temporal drift or measurement uncertainty. Bootstrap widths quantify
sensitivity-estimator variation under the chosen design, not confidence
in a plant parameter. The screening experiment uses finite budgets and
cached distinct-state counters. It has no wall-clock instrumentation,
and the observed increase in exact calls is a negative result for this
suite, not a universal claim about all surrogates or optimizers. These
boundaries constrain interpretation until time-aligned plant data,
prospective holdouts and an independently reviewed deployment protocol
are available.

OreFlow is a steady-state deterministic simulator with authored ore and
equipment parameters. It does not model plant transients, sensor delay,
control logic, froth stability, equipment failure, maintenance, reagent
cost, energy tariffs, concentrate penalties, capital expenditure or mine
scheduling. Its optimizer therefore returns a finite simulator decision,
not an economic optimum or operating recommendation. The twelve cases
are research scenarios rather than a probability sample of mines, and
their shared equations create structural dependence. The 3,072 learning
rows test transfer within this authored domain. The GeoMet samples lack
time-aligned plant controls and equipment states, preventing attribution
of the large mechanistic bias to one physical cause. The particle
benchmark uses constructed probabilities rather than observed liberation
labels. The uncertainty analysis assumes independent factors over
authored boxes and does not encode geological covariance or temporal
drift. The screening counters exclude inference and elapsed time; the
measured increase in exact calls is limited to this suite and budget.

\section{Conclusions}\label{conclusions}

OreFlow provides a reproducible steady-state process engine and an
explicit set of learning, optimization and uncertainty experiments over
twelve authored mineral-processing cases. Its strongest evidence is
computationally bounded: the Python engine closes the modeled balances,
the TypeScript workbench reproduces the committed states within declared
tolerances, and the report preserves misses against published or
measured references rather than treating them as calibration. None of
these checks converts the authored circuits into plant-calibrated
models.

The learning results show why validation unit matters. Interpolation
performance can coexist with a rejected magnetite fold, seed instability
and negative grouped-transfer scores for log upgrade. A score averaged
over admitted folds must retain its denominator and cannot be presented
as performance on the excluded ore. The optimizer comparison is also
negative for its stated goal: in 72 paired target-mode variants, its
learned screen increased recorded cached engine evaluations by 17.25\%,
with more evaluations in 68 cases, ties in four and savings in none.
Similar feasible endpoints do not negate that cost result. The
uncertainty analysis describes output variation inside authored
independent-factor boxes; its Sobol bootstrap widths are conservative
diagnostics rather than calibrated confidence intervals.

Taken together, the product is a research workbench for inspecting
mechanistic assumptions and evaluation design. It is not a plant digital
twin, an economic optimizer or an operating recommendation system. The
next scientifically useful step is not to broaden claims from these
finite records; it is to acquire time-aligned plant metallurgy, process
controls and equipment data, define prospective ore and production-period holdouts,
calibrate only on designated periods, and compare against untouched
periods with uncertainty and a documented safety review. Until those
data exist, OreFlow provides transparent computation, not
a claim of industrial prediction.

\section{Reproducibility}\label{reproducibility}

The repository is https://github.com/fsantibanezleal/CAOS\_OreFlow
(MIT). The engine, the bake, the records, the browser port, the service
and the documentation are versioned together; the source, records and
computational environment are versioned with the software release.
\texttt{./scripts/setup.ps1} builds the environments and
\texttt{./scripts/precompute.ps1} regenerates every record. The
0.11.000 validation record reports 6,005 s for the twelve cases on
twelve workers, 4,002 s for the learning stage, 550 s for studies and 292 s
for real-sample processing. The learning design, all fifteen evaluation jobs
and all 65 neural-seed jobs were executed fresh, with no resumed
checkpoints. The learned stage records CUDA execution on an RTX 4070
Laptop GPU. Its fit and evaluation concern authored simulator cases;
this computation does not establish plant-calibrated performance. Independent
evaluation folds and neural seed runs can execute in bounded spawned
workers. External checkpoints are accepted only under the same
scientific source, JSON constants, release version, case definitions,
operating contract, settings, dependency versions and compute-runtime
fingerprint. Each checkpoint has a verified JSON checksum and scientific
diagnostics; corrupt or foreign records fail rather than being silently
reused. Execution metadata distinguishes planned, computed and resumed
jobs. Final deployed-model fitting and ONNX parity checks were executed
in the completed learned stage. Checkpoint reuse within that identical
candidate is distinct from the development-only reuse of an earlier
release's learning record, which is rejected for publication.
\texttt{./scripts/smoke.ps1} runs the checks: the guards, the Python and
frontend suites and the build, with the counts of each release in
\texttt{docs/release-verification.md}. The workbench at
https://oreflow.ml.fasl-work.com runs the engine in the browser on any
state of any case.
